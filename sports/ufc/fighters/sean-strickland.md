## Fighting Style

**High-volume boxing pressure fighter with elite chin and cardio**

Strickland is one of the most unusual profiles in middleweight history:

1. **Volume boxing machine** — throws relentless jabs and straight rights, rarely goes for the KO
2. **Durability first** — takes punishment willingly to land his own shots; the attrition strategy
3. **Elite cardio** — his pace does not drop in Rounds 4–5; he is consistently stronger late in fights
4. **No finishing instinct** — actively avoids going for the finish, which costs him in close decisions

### Striking
- Southpaw-adjacent jab despite being orthodox — his long 76" reach makes his jab uniquely effective
- Throws jab-straight combinations at an elite rate (~6 per minute)
- Seldom throws kicks or elbows — almost exclusively a hands fighter
- Power is moderate — 9 of 29 wins by KO/TKO, but most were ground-and-pound or opponent tiring
- **No finishing urgency**: he will land 200 strikes and not try to end the fight — judges love this, but it creates close-decision variance

### Grappling
- Not a takedown threat — very low TD average (0.86 per 15 min)
- Excellent takedown defense (78%) — he prefers to keep fights standing
- When taken down he works back to his feet efficiently
- No submission threat whatsoever

### Cardio
- Arguably top-3 cardio in middleweight history
- His pace in Round 5 is identical to Round 1 — no visible fatigue
- Opponents who pressure him in early rounds often gas out
- This is the primary betting edge: fade opponents who rely on early aggression

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. Alex Pereira — UFC FN 215 (Win, UD5)
Strickland's breakout performance. Fought the Brazilian kickboxing world champion and outboxed him for 5 rounds using jab volume and head movement. Established his elite cardio and durability. Pereira knocked him down once but Strickland walked through it.

### vs. Israel Adesanya — UFC 293 (Win, UD5) — Middleweight Title
The upset of 2023. Strickland was a +300 underdog and dominated "The Last Stylebender" with relentless pressure and volume boxing. Adesanya never found his range — Strickland's aggression and reach made this a one-sided fight. Shocked the MMA world.

### vs. Dricus du Plessis — UFC 297 (Loss, SD5)
Strickland lost the title on a split decision that was extremely controversial. Widely considered a robbery or a 50-50 fight. Strickland landed more total strikes; Du Plessis landed more significant strikes. The close decision exposed his refusal to finish — costs him titles in close fights.

### vs. Dricus du Plessis — UFC 311 (Loss, SD5)
The rematch was equally close. Again went to a split decision loss. Strickland again outworked du Plessis in volume; du Plessis arguably landed the more damaging shots. His inability to close out fights with a finish continues to cost him championship opportunities.

### When to fade Strickland

| Scenario | Lean |
|----------|------|
| Heavy favorite (-220 or worse) in 3-round fight | **Fade** — no finishing instinct = close decision risk |
| Opponent is an elite power puncher (Pereira, Adesanya, Costa) | **Slight fade** — they can hurt him |
| Method market: Back him by KO/TKO early | **Fade** — he will not pursue the finish |
| Decision market close to even (-110) | **Neutral or slight fade** — his refusal to finish makes decisions a coin flip |
| Opponent is a wrestler (rare MW grappler) | **Fade on decision** — if taken down repeatedly, judges notice |

### Method of Victory Markets
- **Decision**: His bread-and-butter — 20 of 29 wins by decision (69%)
- **KO/TKO**: Possible but rare and typically from ground-and-pound or accumulated damage — avoid early KO props
- **Submission**: Has never submitted a UFC opponent — hard fade on this prop

### Totals
- **Back Over 2.5 rounds** in any Strickland fight — he has gone the distance in nearly every fight
- In 5-round fights, **Over 3.5** is value — he has never been finished before Round 3 in a meaningful fight
- Only fade the over if his opponent is an elite finisher who can catch him cold (Pereira types)

### Line Value Notes
- Books consistently underprice Strickland as a dog — his cardio and chin make him competitive in almost every fight
- His +300 line against Adesanya was the UFC bet of the year in 2023
- When Strickland is listed +200 or higher, research the matchup closely — the public underrates him
