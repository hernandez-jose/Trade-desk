# Stage 2: Business Plan & Funding

**Timeline:** Months 2–3
**Goal:** Build a realistic financial model, secure funding, and have a document that can survive scrutiny from a bank, landlord, or investor.

---

## Why the Business Plan Matters (Even If You're Self-Funding)

A business plan forces you to confront the numbers before the market does. Restaurants fail not from bad food but from bad math. Even if no one else reads it, building the financial model will reveal whether the concept works before you spend $300K finding out the hard way.

Landlords for good locations will ask for it. Banks require it. SBA lenders won't talk to you without it.

---

## Business Plan Structure

### 1. Executive Summary (1–2 pages)
- Concept description (from your Stage 1 one-pager)
- Market opportunity
- Funding request and use of funds
- Summary financial projections (Year 1, Year 2, Year 3)
- Owner background

### 2. Company Description
- Legal entity type (LLC recommended — see below)
- Ownership structure
- Mission statement
- Concept overview

### 3. Market Analysis
- San Jose restaurant market size and trends
- Target customer personas
- Competitive analysis (direct and indirect)
- Your competitive advantage

### 4. Operations Plan
- Location (or target criteria if not yet identified)
- Facility requirements
- Kitchen design overview
- Hours of operation
- Technology stack (POS, AI back office, delivery integrations)
- Staffing model

### 5. Marketing Plan (summary — full detail in Stage 9)
- Brand positioning
- Launch strategy
- Customer acquisition channels
- Ongoing marketing approach and budget

### 6. Financial Plan (the most important section)
- Startup cost breakdown
- Monthly operating budget
- Revenue projections (3 scenarios: conservative, base, optimistic)
- Cash flow forecast (24 months minimum)
- Break-even analysis
- Funding sources and repayment plan

### 7. Appendices
- Owner resumes
- Letters of intent from vendors (if any)
- Preliminary lease letters of intent
- Menu draft
- Any concept validation data from Stage 1

## Financial Model: Build It Yourself with AI Assistance

Use Claude to build your financial model from first principles. Feed it your concept details and San Jose cost assumptions and generate a 24-month cash flow model.

### Key Assumptions to Lock In

**Revenue assumptions:**
- Operating days per year (plan for 350; buffer for closures, holidays)
- Peak vs. off-peak split (lunch/dinner/weekend)
- Average check per daypart
- Table turns per service
- Catering revenue (build in separately — it has different margins)
- Delivery % of total revenue (higher in SJ; 25–40% is common)

**Cost assumptions:**
- Food cost % by category (28–32% blended target)
- Labor % target (28–35%; lean heavily on AI to stay at the low end)
- Rent (use a specific location you're targeting or an assumption based on neighborhood)
- Utilities ($1,500–$4,000/month depending on concept)
- Delivery platform fees (25–30% of delivery revenue)
- Software/AI stack (~$1,200/month — see ai-playbook.md)
- Marketing ($1,500–$3,000/month)

**The math that matters:**
```
Gross Revenue
- Food Cost (target: 30%)
- Labor Cost (target: 30%)
= Gross Profit (target: 40%)

- Rent & Occupancy (target: <10% of revenue)
- Utilities
- Marketing
- Software
- Insurance
- Accounting
= EBITDA (target: 10–15% in year 2+)
```

## Legal Entity Setup

### Recommended: Single-Member or Multi-Member LLC
- Protects personal assets from business liabilities (critical in California)
- Pass-through taxation (avoid double taxation)
- Relatively simple to set up ($70 California filing fee + $800 minimum franchise tax)
- Add an Operating Agreement even if you're the sole owner

### If Taking on Investors
Consider a Delaware C-Corp or CA Corp if you're raising equity from multiple investors. Talk to a startup attorney (many offer flat-fee restaurant startup packages).

### EIN & Bank Account
- Get EIN from IRS.gov (free, takes 10 minutes)
- Open a dedicated business checking account immediately — never mix personal and business
- Recommended: Mercury (online, no fees, great integrations) or Chase Business (for SBA relationships)

## Funding Strategy

### Bootstrap Path (Preferred for First-Time Operators)
1. **Personal savings:** $30K–$100K — your skin in the game
2. **Friends & family round:** $20K–$100K — soft terms, investor agreements still required
3. **SBA Microloan ($50K max):** Faster than 7(a), good for ghost kitchen launch
4. **Revenue-fund next stage:** Use ghost kitchen cash flow to fund fast-casual build-out

### SBA Loan Path (For Brick-and-Mortar)
**SBA 7(a) Loan:**
- Amount: $150K–$500K
- Term: 10–25 years
- Rate: Prime + 2.75% (currently ~9–11%)
- Requires: 2+ years business history OR strong personal credit (700+) + collateral
- Timeline: 60–90 days from application to funding
- Best banks in Bay Area for SBA restaurant loans: Wells Fargo SBA, Live Oak Bank, Celtic Bank

**What lenders want to see:**
- Business plan with 3-year financials
- Personal financial statement
- 6 months bank statements
- Proof of industry experience (your restaurant work history or management background)
- Collateral (home equity commonly used)

### Crowdfunding (Community + Marketing)
- **Mainvest:** Restaurant-specific; investors get revenue share (not equity)
- **Honeycomb Credit:** Similar; good for neighborhood/community concepts
- Dual benefit: raises money AND creates invested customers who become your ambassadors
- Typical raise: $50K–$300K; expect 6–9% annualized revenue share

### What to Avoid
- **High-interest MCAs (Merchant Cash Advances):** Effective APR of 40–150%; dangerous for cash flow
- **Personal credit cards for large purchases:** Use only for small, quickly repaid expenses
- **Giving away equity too early:** A $50K investment for 20% equity is almost always a bad deal at this stage
