# Melsik Baghdasaryan — Deep Fight Research
## UFC Fight Night: Kape vs. Horiguchi (UFC Vegas 119) | vs. Murtazali Magomedov | June 20, 2026 | 145 lbs | 3 Rounds (Prelims)

> Analyst note: A 34-year-old decorated kickboxer with elite southpaw power but a documented hole on the ground (two submission losses, 0% offensive wrestling), now coming off a R1 KO loss and a ~16-month layoff, drawn against a 10-0 finish-everything Dagestani grappler. His only realistic win is a clean KO before it hits the mat; the matchup steers toward his worst area.
> Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR Baghdasaryan:** He is the more proven striker by a wide margin — a former Wu Lin Feng champion and K-1-level kickboxer with genuine southpaw KO power (5 of 8 wins by KO/TKO). His opponent has zero UFC tape, has never been past the early rounds, and is untested against elite striking. If Baghdasaryan can keep it standing and land first, he can starch a debutant. **AGAINST Baghdasaryan:** His defensive grappling is a liability — both of his career submission losses (incl. an RNC to Josh Culibao) came after being grounded, he has 0% takedown offense, and ~57% takedown defense is mediocre. He is 34, coming off a brutal R1 TKO loss to Jean Silva, and has been inactive ~16 months. Magomedov's whole game is taking people down and finishing them. **Most likely path:** Magomedov gets it to the mat and finishes — Baghdasaryan's win requires a first-strike KO that the market (and matchup) say is the lower-probability outcome.

*(Sources: Wikipedia, ESPN, Sherdog, UFC.com)*

## 3. Opponent Context
Magomedov (10-0, 100% finishes, 5 KO / 5 sub) is precisely the archetype that has beaten Baghdasaryan before: a grappler who wants the takedown and the submission. Baghdasaryan has no offensive wrestling to threaten Magomedov and a documented inability to get up once grounded. Magomedov also has R1 KO power, so even a standing exchange is not risk-free for an aging striker with 56% striking defense.

## 4. Striking Analysis
### Offense
Elite kickboxing pedigree: southpaw straight left, hard kicks, K-1-caliber striking. 5.45 SLpM cited in some sources; UFCStats-era ~4.00 SLpM at 60% accuracy. This is his entire path to victory.
### Defense
Mediocre for a "striker": ~56% striking defense, ~4.00 SApM (absorbs about as much as he lands). Chin was cracked by Jean Silva (R1 TKO). At 34, durability is a question.
### Historical Striking Data
SLpM ~4.00, accuracy ~60% (119/199), SApM ~4.00, defense ~56% (UFCStats via search summaries; small 5-fight UFC sample).

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: First-strike KO (moderate-low likelihood)
Catch the debutant early with the southpaw left/kick before grappling exchanges accumulate. His best and most realistic route.
### Path 2: Sprawl-and-bang decision (low likelihood)
Stuff takedowns all night and out-strike — but ~57% TDD vs. a Dagestani-style grappler makes a clean 15 minutes unlikely.
### Path 3: Opponent gasses (low likelihood)
If Magomedov (no deep-water tape) fades after a hard R1, Baghdasaryan's superior late composure could swing R2–R3. Speculative.

## 13. Risk Factors & Confidence Assessment

1. Gets taken down and submitted (his proven failure mode).
2. Eats early power and is finished standing (56% striking defense, recent KO loss).
3. Rust/age leave him a step slow vs. a 26-year-old finisher.

### Confidence Level
Model win-probability estimate for Baghdasaryan: **~32%** (see `analysis/mma-fight-prediction.md`). The style matchup steers into his worst area; his only equity is a puncher's chance plus the genuine uncertainty of betting a 10-0 fighter with no UFC tape priced at −290. That uncertainty is why this lands as a dog with a live KO prop rather than a confident fade — but it is a fade of his moneyline at current prices.
