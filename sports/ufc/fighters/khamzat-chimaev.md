## Fighting Style

**Elite wrestling-based destroyer with finishing ability across all positions**

Chimaev is built around four pillars:
1. **Explosive level changes** — shot speed is elite; opponents rarely see the takedown coming
2. **Smothering top control** — once he gets you down, you are not getting up or escaping
3. **Arm drag / body lock** — preferred entry to the takedown; transitions to clinch seamlessly
4. **Sharp counters standing** — not a pure boxer but times shots well and has underrated power

### Grappling
- Background in wrestling and Jiu-Jitsu from Sweden's Allstars Training Center
- Takedown accuracy of ~75% is historically rare at any level in the UFC
- Dominant ground-and-pound from top position — not just a "lay and pray" wrestler
- Rear naked choke is his finishing submission; transitions to the back quickly
- Submission defense is elite — he controls opponents before they can threaten

### Striking
- More powerful than his wrestling-first reputation suggests
- Tight combinations off the jab; lands the straight right behind the jab well
- Uses strikes to set up takedowns (like Covington) but with more genuine KO threat
- **Vulnerability exposed**: Burns landed heavy shots in Round 1 of UFC 273, including a knockdown — shows chin can be tested by elite power
- Prefers pressure to counter-striking; walks opponents into the fence

### Durability / Pressure
- Has never been finished in his professional career
- Burns test (UFC 273) showed he can take big shots and recover — this is a positive sign for bettors backing him
- Pace is elite but fights end early, so cardio is rarely truly stress-tested

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. Gilbert Burns — UFC 273 (Win, UD3)
Chimaev's defining performance — and his biggest scare. Burns, a top-5 WW and BJJ black belt, landed a right hand early in Round 1 that buckled Chimaev. Instead of falling apart, Chimaev recovered, took Burns down, and controlled the next two rounds. The decision win showed both his heart and his ability to problem-solve under pressure. **Betting takeaway**: he is not invincible on the feet, but he is resilient.

### vs. Li Jingliang — UFC 267 (Win, Sub R1)
Domination from start to finish. Clinch control, body lock takedown, rear naked choke in round one. The blueprint for how dominant he can be when the wrestling gap is wide. Reinforced the "early finish" prop markets.

### vs. Kevin Holland — UFC 279 (Win, Sub R1)
After missing weight and the original Diaz main event fell apart, Chimaev submitted Holland in round one. Less notable for the fight itself and more for the weight miss — it signals that cutting to 170 may not be sustainable long-term.

### vs. Kamaru Usman — UFC 294 (Win, TKO R3)
A statement win over a former UFC champion. Chimaev hurt Usman and stopped him in Round 3. Established himself as a genuine title contender at welterweight. Clean performance with minimal damage absorbed.

| Scenario | Lean |

### Method of Victory Markets
- **Submission**: Strong value — he regularly sinks the rear naked choke; best prop market for Chimaev
- **KO/TKO**: Also available but slightly less likely than submission; still a viable prop
- **Decision**: He almost never goes to the scorecards — fade decision props
- **Early finish (R1/R2)**: Back the early round props; his average fight time is under 8 minutes

### Totals
- Back **Under 1.5 rounds** when the price is reasonable — he finishes half his fights in Round 1
- **Under 2.5 rounds** is the safer over — nearly all his fights end before the third
- When he faces an elite defensive opponent, adjust to **Under 3.5** as a hedge
