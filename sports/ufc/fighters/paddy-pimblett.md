## Fighting Style

**Aggressive pressure grappler with heavy submission offense and developing striking**

Pimblett is built around two pillars: pulling guard or dragging opponents to the ground via clinch, and finishing submissions from bottom or top position. In the UFC, he has increasingly shown the ability to use movement and striking to set up his grappling, but remains most dangerous when controlling the clinch range or working from his back. He possesses a 2nd-degree BJJ black belt and is one of the most technically polished submission artists in the lightweight division. His fighting style is high-risk, high-reward: he will accept punishment to get the finish.

### Grappling
- 2nd-degree BJJ black belt; trained from age 15 at Next Generation Liverpool
- Submission diversity is exceptional: rear naked chokes, flying triangles, triangle armbars, leg locks, anaconda chokes
- Effective at pulling guard from standing or dragging opponents down via dirty boxing
- Dangerous from the bottom — many opponents have been caught trying to advance on him
- Guard is active and offensive; rarely just stalls on the bottom
- Takedown accuracy is limited (~28%) — prefers to clinch and trip rather than shoot doubles
- Takedown defense is underrated; opponents generally do not want to engage his guard

### Striking
- Orthodox stance; throws with aggression rather than technical precision
- Chin is elevated — a known liability identified by analysts at the elite level (Gaethje fight)
- Has real KO power: seven professional KO wins, including Vendramini (UFC debut) and Chandler (UFC 314)
- Uses leg kicks and body shots to set up clinch entries
- Combination striking is improving but still exploitable against elite counter-punchers
- Best striking comes in mid-range transition — the moment an opponent reaches to clinch, Pimblett pivots to submission attempts
- Weak against fighters who maintain distance and pick shots (Gaethje model)

### Cardio
Pimblett's conditioning has developed significantly. His UFC fights through UFC 314 were mostly short (R1 finishes or 3-round decisions he controlled). The Gaethje fight at UFC 324 was his first five-round appearance — he absorbed significant volume but survived all 25 minutes. His cardio did not collapse, but his output and accuracy dropped sharply from rounds 3–5, suggesting he is better suited to 3-round formats where his finishing instincts are sharpest.

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. Luigi Vendramini — UFC FN London (Win, KO R1, Sep 2021)
Pimblett's UFC debut. Entering with enormous UK hype but limited UFC experience, he was wobbled early by Vendramini before storming back to land a brutal finish in Round 1. Established him as a legitimate finisher and not just a grappling specialist. Showed the heart and chin to absorb early adversity.

### vs. Jordan Leavitt — UFC FN London (Win, Sub R2, Jul 2022)
Pimblett suffered a rough first round — taken down, mounted, ground-and-pounded — before rallying in Round 2 and submitting Leavitt with a rear naked choke. The most telling proof of his resilience and BJJ ceiling: he finished a fight after being dominated off his own game plan.

### vs. Jared Gordon — UFC 282 (Win, UD3, Dec 2022)
The most controversial decision of Pimblett's UFC run. Many press row scorecards had Gordon winning, and the outcry marked the first serious public debate about whether Pimblett was benefiting from favorable judging. Important for betting: Pimblett can look worse than his result suggests in fights where he fails to finish.

### vs. Michael Chandler — UFC 314 (Win, TKO R3, Apr 2025)
The signature win of Pimblett's career. Dominated a former world champion in Chandler across all three rounds — cut him with a knee, controlled on the ground, and TKO'd him with a third-round barrage. Showcased his complete game and silenced doubters who questioned his elite ceiling. Set up the Gaethje title shot.

### vs. Justin Gaethje — UFC 324 (Loss, UD5, Jan 2026) — Interim Lightweight Title
The first truly elite opponent Pimblett faced, and the first real setback of his UFC career. Pimblett took enormous punishment across five rounds but showed undeniable heart in surviving. Lost clearly on most scorecards (48-47, 49-46, 49-46). Critical data point for bettors: Pimblett's chin elevation and game plan rigidity were exposed against a high-level counter puncher. He attempted only 5 takedowns all fight — when his primary weapon was neutralized, he had no clear Plan B.

### When to fade Pimblett

| Scenario | Lean |
|----------|------|
| vs. elite counter puncher who can maintain distance | **Fade** — Gaethje blueprint works |
| 5-round fight at championship level | **Fade or neutral** — output drops late, game plan issues exposed |
| Heavy UFC favorite (-250 or worse) | **Fade on the price** — he can lose decisions he dominates on activity |
| vs. elite wrestler with sub defense (Tsarukyan, Oliveira-type) | **Fade** — takedown to top control can neutralize his guard game |
| After a decision win that looked worse than the scorecard | **Fade** — judging benefit may not repeat |
| Opponent with strong reach and jab game | **Fade** — he struggles to close distance cleanly |

### Method of Victory Markets
- **Submission**: Best value in any Pimblett fight. His 1.7 sub avg per 15 min is among the highest in the lightweight division. Back him via sub at significant plus money in most match-ups.
- **KO/TKO**: Real possibility (7 career KOs) but usually comes after grappling scrambles, not clean striking exchanges. Check the number — overpriced in main event spots.
- **Decision**: Pimblett wins decisions but often looks worse than his scorecard. Has benefited from favorable judging (Gordon fight). Fade the decision prop at heavy price.
- **Opponent by KO/TKO**: Worth considering against elite strikers. His 48% striking defense and elevated chin are genuine targets.

### Totals
- **Under 1.5 rounds**: Value when Pimblett is facing lower-ranked grapplers — 12 first-round finishes in his career
- **Over 2.5 rounds**: Strong lean when facing elite strikers who can maintain distance (Gaethje model, Saint Denis)
- **Goes the distance**: Back when the opponent has elite wrestling or clinch control that prevents Pimblett from implementing his submission game
