## Fighting Style

Sandhagen is a lanky, cerebral switch-stance striker who blends unorthodox angles, Muay Thai, kickboxing, and boxing into a difficult-to-read offensive system. He is one of the most creative and unpredictable offensive fighters in the bantamweight division, with elite footwork, head movement, and the ability to generate power from unexpected positions. His willingness to throw spinning techniques, flying knees, and wheel kicks makes him a constant highlight-reel threat. Despite a collegiate wrestling background at the University of Northern Colorado, Sandhagen is primarily identified as a striker and spends most fights upright. He has elite volume and places immense pressure on opponents with his pace.

### Grappling

Sandhagen has a credible wrestling base from his college days but it is not his primary weapon. His takedown defense (63–64%) is below elite level and has been his most exploited vulnerability — Merab Dvalishvili (20 takedowns in 5 rounds), Umar Nurmagomedov, and Aljamain Sterling all dominated him on the ground. When he chooses to use takedowns offensively, he showed surprising effectiveness against Rob Font (a takedown in each of five rounds). His submission offense is rare — he has 3 submission wins in his career but averages only 0.3 attempts per 15 minutes at UFC level. When taken down, he has solid scramble ability and is dangerous off his back with submissions from guard, but prolonged grappling is a losing proposition for him.

### Striking

This is Sandhagen's elite attribute. He lands 4.86 significant strikes per minute with 45% accuracy — high volume for bantamweight. His switch stance creates constant alignment problems for opponents, and he times his transitions mid-combination. Signature weapons include a snapping jab from range, front kicks to the body, spinning back-fist, spinning heel kick (finished Moraes), flying knee (finished Edgar in 0:28 — second-fastest flying knee KO in UFC history), and opportunistic elbows in the clinch. He is a natural pressure fighter who initiates offense from distance and stays busy throughout fights. He tends to fight effectively moving backward, using his reach to land counters. His striking defense (56–58%) is respectable but he can be hurt — Yan dropped him, and Dillashaw hurt him multiple times.

### Cardio

Cardio is a clear strength. Sandhagen maintains output across 25-minute fights consistently. In his five-round wins over Vera, Font, and Moraes he showed no significant fade. He fights at a high pace and still lands clean strikes late in fights. Has not been stopped due to fatigue. His losses have all been to superior wrestling or sharp countering, not gassing out.

## Career Defining Fights

**1. vs. Merab Dvalishvili — UFC 320 (Oct 4, 2025) — LOSS, UD (49-45, 49-45, 49-46)**
Sandhagen's failed championship bid against the relentless Dvalishvili exposed the ceiling on his mixed game. Dvalishvili broke the UFC bantamweight title fight record with 20 takedowns across 5 rounds, neutralizing Sandhagen's striking completely. The fight illustrated the hard truth about Sandhagen's profile: elite wrestlers who can pressure him for 25 minutes will win comfortably. He was competitive early but could not solve the pace.

**2. vs. Frankie Edgar — UFC Fight Night 184 (Feb 6, 2021) — WIN, KO, Rd 1 (0:28)**
The flying knee KO of Edgar is the signature moment of Sandhagen's career — second-fastest flying knee finish in UFC history, and Combat Press 2021 Knockout of the Year. He feinted a punch, stepped in, and launched a right flying knee that put Edgar face-first on the canvas. Demonstrated elite timing and creativity at the highest level.

**3. vs. Petr Yan — UFC 267 (Oct 30, 2021) — LOSS, UD (49-46 x3)**
The interim bantamweight title fight against Yan is the best five-round fight Sandhagen has been in. He was competitive through the first two rounds but Yan's spinning back-fist in Round 3 turned the tide. The unanimous 49-46 scorecards show how decisively Yan pulled away. This fight showed Sandhagen can compete with elite opponents but also that elite strikers who are also physically stronger will find openings on him.

**4. vs. Marlon Moraes — UFC Fight Night 179 (Oct 11, 2020) — WIN, TKO, Rd 2 (1:03)**
This fight announced Sandhagen to a wider audience. He absorbed heavy early offense from Moraes — a feared knockout artist — before timing a spinning heel kick in Round 2 to drop and finish him. Showed his ability to absorb punishment, stay calm, and generate devastating counterattacks from unconventional techniques. Performance of the Night bonus.

**5. vs. TJ Dillashaw — UFC on ESPN (Jul 24, 2021) — LOSS, SD (48-47, 48-47, 47-48)**
A controversial split decision loss that many observers thought Sandhagen deserved to win. Dillashaw returned from a two-year drug suspension and edged a closely contested five-rounder. Sandhagen's tendency to coast in perceived winning rounds — a repeated criticism — likely cost him this fight on the cards. The lesson: he cannot afford to relax in championship rounds when margins are thin.

### Method of Victory Markets
Sandhagen is one of the most viable KO/TKO plays in the bantamweight division. He throws spinning techniques, flying knees, and wheel kicks that finish fights suddenly. His KO/TKO rate: 8 of 18 wins (44%). His early-round finish rate is notable — the Edgar KO (0:28), the Moraes TKO (Rd 2), the Figueiredo TKO (Rd 2) all came before the third round. Against striking-oriented opponents, backing Sandhagen by KO/TKO typically offers value between +150 and +250. Avoid the decision market when he faces wrestlers — those fights either end in Sandhagen's favor by grinding decision when he survives, or the wrestler controls to unanimous decision.

### Totals
Sandhagen fights tend to go long. His last five fights: 5 rounds (Dvalishvili), 2 rounds (Figueiredo), 5 rounds (Nurmagomedov), 5 rounds (Font), 5 rounds (Vera). Against wrestlers and grapplers, OVER is the strong lean because the fight becomes a wrestling attrition match with limited finishing action. Against pure strikers, both UNDER and OVER have value — he finishes around 44% of wins by stoppage but also grinds out decisions. The Moraes and Edgar finishes both came early (under 2 rounds). Generally, favor OVER in fights where Sandhagen faces a wrestler; lean UNDER when he faces a light-hitting striker he should dominate on the feet.
