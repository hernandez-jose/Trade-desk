# Stage 9: Marketing & Brand

**Timeline:** Months 7–11 (brand locked by Month 7; active marketing from Month 9)
**Goal:** Build a brand that San Jose customers recognize, trust, and recommend — using AI to scale content output without a marketing department.

---

## Brand First, Tactics Second

Marketing tactics (Instagram posts, ads, Yelp) only work if they point back to a clear brand. A brand is not a logo — it's the answer to: "Why would someone choose you over the 30 other options within 1 mile?" Lock the brand identity before spending a dollar on marketing.

---

## Brand Identity Framework

### 4 Questions to Define Your Brand
1. **Positioning:** Who is this for and what do we do better than anyone else for them?
2. **Voice:** If the restaurant were a person, how would it talk? (Warm? Irreverent? Expert? Casual?)
3. **Values:** What do we actually stand for? (Freshness? Community? Authenticity? Speed?)
4. **Promise:** What can customers reliably expect every single time they come in?

**Write a one-sentence brand positioning statement:**
"For [target customer], [restaurant name] is the [category] that delivers [key benefit] because [reason to believe]."

Example: "For San Jose tech workers who want lunch that's actually worth leaving the office for, Kilo is the fast casual that serves chef-quality grain bowls in under 5 minutes because we prep everything fresh every morning."

Use Claude to workshop your positioning statement. Feed in your concept one-pager from Stage 1 and ask for 10 variations, then edit.

### Visual Identity (Minimum Viable Brand)
You need these before launch:
- **Logo** (wordmark or icon+wordmark) — Use Canva AI or hire a local designer for $500–$1,500
- **Color palette** (2 primary + 1 accent; save HEX codes)
- **Typography** (1–2 fonts; use Google Fonts for consistency and cost)
- **Photography style** (warm and moody? Bright and clean? Rustic? Decide before your first food photos)
- **Brand guidelines document** (1–2 pages; Claude can generate from your decisions)

Everything — menu, signage, social, packaging, website — must use the same palette, fonts, and photo style. Inconsistency signals amateurism.

## Pre-Opening Marketing (Months 7–10)

### Building the Audience Before Day One
The goal: have 500+ engaged followers and 100+ email addresses before your first day of service. These people become your opening-day crowd and your first review writers.

**Tactics:**
- **Start Instagram 8–10 weeks before opening** — "Coming soon" content: space progress, kitchen equipment arrivals, recipe development, team introductions
- **Build-out content series** — Weekly progress photos and videos. The audience grows fastest when there's a story unfolding.
- **Email capture** — "Be the first to know" landing page (use Mailchimp or Klaviyo, free tier is enough to start). Offer: "Sign up for a free drink at soft open."
- **Google Business Profile** — Claim and set up immediately. Add photos, hours (listed as "opening soon"), and your website. This drives SEO from day one.
- **Yelp page** — Claim and set up. Add photos and concept description before any reviews come in.

### Pre-Opening AI Content Calendar
Start 8 weeks before open; post 3–4x/week:

| Week | Content Theme |
|---|---|
| Week 8 | Announce you're coming; introduce the concept |
| Week 7 | Behind the scenes: kitchen build-out progress |
| Week 6 | Introduce the team (owner story, chef story) |
| Week 5 | Ingredient spotlight: key ingredient in your concept |
| Week 4 | Menu teaser: first dish reveal |
| Week 3 | Menu teaser: second dish reveal; email signup CTA |
| Week 2 | "Opening soon" countdown; share the story |
| Week 1 | Soft open invite; soft open recap; hours announcement |

Use Claude to generate all captions and scripts in your brand voice. You shoot the photos and video; Claude writes the words.

## Core Marketing Channels

### Google Business Profile (Priority #1)
This is free and drives the most foot traffic for restaurants. Customers searching "best tacos near me" or "lunch San Jose" see your profile before your website.

**Optimize fully:**
- Complete profile: name, address, phone, hours, website, category ("Restaurant," "Fast Casual," etc.)
- Add 20+ photos (exterior, interior, food, team, menu)
- Enable online ordering link (link to your direct ordering system, not DoorDash)
- Post weekly updates (use AI to generate these in 2 minutes)
- Respond to every review within 24 hours (AI drafts, you approve)
- Enable Q&A; answer your own questions to seed common inquiries

**Result:** A fully optimized GBP typically drives 50–100 additional covers per month vs. an incomplete profile.

### Instagram
The dominant discovery channel for restaurants in San Jose's demographic.

**Content types that work:**
- Food photography (90% of your feed should make people hungry)
- Behind-the-scenes (builds connection and trust)
- Staff/team content (humanizes the brand)
- Customer content (repost UGC with permission)
- Seasonal specials and announcements

**Posting frequency:** 4–5 Reels/week + 5–7 Stories/week. Consistency beats virality.

**AI production pipeline:**
- Owner shoots content on iPhone (learn basic food photography: natural light, top-down, 45° angle)
- Claude generates caption + hashtags
- Canva edits the image or creates a Story graphic
- Buffer schedules the post

**Budget for paid Instagram ads:** $500–$1,000/month in the first 3 months (target: 5-mile radius around your location, interest: "Food," "Restaurants," San Jose geo)

### Yelp
San Jose has high Yelp usage. A 4.0+ rating with 50+ reviews is a significant competitive asset.

**Strategy:**
- Never ask for reviews directly (Yelp ToS violation; can filter your reviews)
- Instead: create an experience that organically generates reviews ("Your meal was on us; we'd love to see you again!")
- Respond to every review — especially 1 and 2 stars; AI can generate empathetic responses for owner approval
- Set up Yelp Reservations or connect OpenTable for booking integration
- Yelp ads: $300–$600/month in the San Jose market; track conversions carefully

### TikTok / Reels (High Upside, Especially for Launch)
Short-form video is the highest ROI content format for restaurant discovery in 2025.

**Content that gets organic reach:**
- Day-in-the-life of opening a restaurant
- Recipe or technique reveals
- "How we make [your signature dish]" — process videos consistently perform
- "What $20 gets you at [restaurant name]" — price transparency drives trust
- Customer reaction videos (with permission)

**AI assist:** CapCut's AI auto-editing + Claude-generated scripts for voiceover or on-screen text.

### Email Marketing
The only channel you own. Algorithms change; your email list doesn't.

**Building the list:**
- Soft open sign-up ("Get exclusive access") — target 100+ before opening
- Point of sale collection: "Join our rewards?" — ask at checkout
- Post-visit follow-up (via reservation system)

**Email cadence:**
- Welcome series (3 emails, automated): Welcome → Your first visit guide → Special offer for second visit
- Weekly: Specials of the week (2–3 sentences + photo; AI generates in 60 seconds)
- Monthly: Newsletter + menu updates
- Triggered: Birthday offer, 60-day lapse reactivation

**Avoid:** Emailing more than 2x/week. Restaurant emails that over-send get unsubscribed.

### Delivery Platforms (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub)

Delivery platforms are a customer acquisition channel that charges you 25–30% commission. Treat them as marketing, not revenue.

**Strategy:**
- List on DoorDash and Uber Eats on day 1 (they drive discovery)
- Use platform promotions in months 1–3 to drive early orders and reviews
- Simultaneously push all customers to your **direct ordering link** (via Toast, Square, or your website) — same food, 0% commission
- Goal: move delivery revenue from 25–30% commission to <5% in-house commission over 12 months

**Packaging as marketing:**
- Every delivery order leaves your restaurant with your branding on it
- Include a card: "Thank you for your order! Order directly at [website] and save — we'll give you [10% off / free drink]" — this is legal and effective

### Corporate Catering Marketing (San Jose's Highest-Margin Channel)

This is unique to San Jose's market. One regular corporate client is worth 3–5 months of individual orders.

**How to acquire corporate clients:**
1. Identify 20 companies within 2 miles; get to the office manager or "food committee"
2. Offer a free trial lunch for 10–15 people ("no commitment, just try us")
3. Follow up with a proposal and menu within 24 hours (AI generates the proposal)
4. Set a monthly account check-in with the client contact

**Channels:**
- LinkedIn outreach to Operations Managers, Office Managers, Executive Assistants at target companies
- EZCater — Catering marketplace; 15–20% commission but massive reach; use to test and acquire, then convert to direct
- Your own catering inquiry form + AI quote pipeline (see ai-playbook.md)

## Review Management

### The Review Acquisition System
1. Guest has a great experience
2. Server or cashier mentions: "If you enjoyed your meal, we'd love a review on Google or Yelp!"
3. SMS follow-up sent automatically 2 hours after reservation (via OpenTable/Resy integration)
4. Email thank-you sent next morning with review link

### Responding to Reviews (AI-Assisted)
**Positive reviews:**
- Claude generates a personalized response using the reviewer's specific compliment
- Owner approves in 2 minutes; posts
- Response makes other potential customers feel seen when they read reviews

**Negative reviews:**
- Claude generates an empathetic, non-defensive response: acknowledge, apologize, invite back
- Never argue, never offer refunds publicly
- The response is marketing — other customers judge your response more than the original complaint

**Target ratings:** 4.3+ on Google; 4.0+ on Yelp within 6 months.

## Launch Event Strategy

### Soft Open (Friends, Family, and Local Influencers)
- 2 weeks before public open
- Invite 30–50 people: friends, family, neighbors, local food bloggers, influencers
- Run service at 50% capacity; use as a training and practice run
- Collect feedback (SurveyMonkey via AI-generated form); fix issues before public open

### Public Opening
- Make it an event: music, discounts, a line outside drives more curiosity
- Invite local press: San Jose Mercury News, Silicon Valley Business Journal, San Jose Eater
- Partner with a local charity for opening week: X% of revenue donated → local press coverage
- Create a signature "Opening Week" dish that's only available for 7 days — drives urgency

### Local Influencer Strategy
- Identify 10–20 San Jose food influencers with 2,000–50,000 followers (micro-influencers outperform macro for local restaurants)
- Invite for a complimentary media meal in exchange for a post (no obligation on either side — most will post if the food is good)
- Use Claude to write outreach DMs that are personalized, brief, and genuine
