## Key Analytical Findings

### Lone'er Kavanagh — Strengths
1. **Elite striking efficiency:** 50% accuracy and 58% defensive rate make him the cleanest technical striker in this matchup — he lands more, gets hit less, every round
2. **Championship-level takedown defense:** 88.9% TDD including a shutout of Moreno's 8 attempts; removes Royval's most reliable path to victory
3. **Ascending trajectory and proven mentality:** Beat a two-time champion on short notice at age 26; mental composure under pressure is confirmed at the highest sub-championship level

### Lone'er Kavanagh — Vulnerabilities
1. **KO susceptibility inside:** Charles Johnson's right hook in R2 exposed that Kavanagh can be stopped when caught with power at close range — Royval creates exactly those messy inside exchanges
2. **Grappling depth unknown against elite ground fighters:** BJJ purple belt is functional for shot-stuffing but an unproven defense against a black belt instructor's submission library once the fight hits the mat
3. **Limited UFC experience at 26:** Only 4 UFC fights; Royval's championship-level seasoning and problem-solving in crisis moments (e.g., R5 vs. Taira reversal) give him an execution edge in adverse situations

### Brandon Royval — Strengths
1. **Elite submission game:** 9 career submissions, BJJ black belt and instructor-level grappler; if any fight goes to the ground, Kavanagh is in genuine danger at every moment
2. **Southpaw angles and scramble creation:** Royval's southpaw stance and feint-heavy style regularly creates the inside exchanges that led to the only finish Kavanagh has ever suffered
3. **26-fight, championship-level experience:** Has beaten Moreno, Kara-France, Taira, and Schnell; won't be phased by the moment and has real solutions when fights go sideways

### Brandon Royval — Vulnerabilities
1. **Striking defense at 43% and two TKO/KO losses:** Absorbs 4.25 sig. strikes/min; against a cleaner striker like Kavanagh landing at 50% accuracy, cumulative damage is a serious fight-ending concern
2. **Back-to-back losses with a first-round KO stoppage:** The Kape TKO (single right hand, R1 at 3:18) raises legitimate questions about chin durability in the late career stage — Kavanagh has finishing power in his hands
3. **Low takedown average and TD defense:** Royval's 39% TD defense means opponents can neutralize his scramble game by taking him down and controlling, and his 0.66 TD average suggests he cannot reliably initiate grappling without opponent cooperation

## Final Verdict

> **Analytics-only conclusion. CES framework.**

Lone'er Kavanagh scores a **CES of 6.98 vs. Royval's 6.60 — a differential of +0.38 in Kavanagh's favor**, translating to approximately **60/40 win probability** for Kavanagh.
