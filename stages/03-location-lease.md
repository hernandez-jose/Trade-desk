# Stage 3: Location & Lease

**Timeline:** Months 3–5
**Goal:** Find a space that fits the concept, negotiate the best possible lease terms, and don't sign until you've done the full due diligence.

---

## Location Is Strategy, Not Just Real Estate

The best restaurant with the wrong location fails. The average restaurant with the right location thrives. Location drives foot traffic, delivery economics, labor pool, and your customer demographic. Never compromise on location to save $500/month in rent.

---

## How to Think About Location in San Jose

### Primary Filters
1. **Is the density right?** Foot traffic or delivery density must support your volume targets
2. **Is the customer in the neighborhood?** Don't open a $28 entree restaurant in a price-sensitive neighborhood
3. **Can you staff it?** Labor pool within 30 minutes by transit/car matters — North SJ has a smaller local labor pool
4. **Does the space fit the concept?** Ventilation, gas capacity, grease trap, ADA — confirm before falling in love with a space

### The 1-Mile Rule
Drive or walk your target location at:
- 7–9 AM (weekday morning traffic)
- 11:30 AM–1:30 PM (lunch peak)
- 5–8 PM (dinner peak)
- 8–11 PM (late night)
- Saturday noon (weekend)

Count the pedestrians. Note where they're coming from and going to. Check Google Maps foot traffic data. Use Placer.ai for demographic and traffic data on specific addresses.

## What to Look for in a Space

### Non-Negotiables
- [ ] **Proper ventilation / Type 1 hood:** Required for cooking with smoke, grease, or high heat. Installing a new hood is $20,000–$60,000+. Find a space that already has one.
- [ ] **Grease trap / grease interceptor:** Required in San Jose. If the prior tenant was a restaurant, it likely has one. Confirm it's properly sized for your operation.
- [ ] **Gas capacity:** Your kitchen equipment requires BTUs. Check available gas capacity — this cannot be easily upgraded.
- [ ] **Electrical:** 200–400 amp 3-phase service for a commercial kitchen. Verify before signing.
- [ ] **ADA compliance:** California is the most litigated state for ADA violations. Confirm or budget for upgrades.
- [ ] **Proper zoning:** Must be zoned for restaurant use (CG, CN, or similar). Verify with City of San Jose Planning.
- [ ] **Parking or transit access:** San Jose is car-dependent outside of downtown; parking matters.

### Strongly Preferred
- Former restaurant space (saves $50K–$200K in build-out costs)
- Grease trap already installed and inspected
- Walk-in cooler and/or freezer already in place
- Loading zone or accessible delivery area
- Natural light (increases dwell time and positive Yelp associations)

### Red Flags
- No previous restaurant use (means full build-out + all permits from scratch)
- Landlord who has never had a restaurant tenant (doesn't understand your needs)
- Location below street level (poor visibility, ADA challenges, delivery friction)
- Visible deferred maintenance (signals a landlord who won't make repairs)
- Month-to-month neighboring vacancies (sign of a declining corridor)

## The Lease: What to Negotiate

A commercial lease is negotiable. Never accept the first offer. Everything below is standard negotiating points — a good commercial real estate attorney can get most of these.

### Lease Structure Types
- **NNN (Triple Net):** Most common for restaurant retail. You pay base rent + property taxes + insurance + CAM (common area maintenance). Add $1.50–$3.00/sqft/month to the base rent when budgeting.
- **Gross Lease:** All-in flat rent; landlord covers taxes and maintenance. Rare in SJ but occasionally available.
- **Modified Gross:** Split the NNN components — better than pure NNN for predictability.

### Critical Lease Terms to Negotiate

**Tenant Improvement (TI) Allowance**
- Landlord pays you a credit toward build-out costs
- Realistic range in San Jose: $30–$80/sqft for a 5-year term
- On a 1,500 sqft space: $45,000–$120,000 off your build-out costs
- Longer lease = more TI allowance leverage. A 10-year term can yield $100–$150/sqft TI.

**Free Rent Period**
- Industry standard: 2–6 months free rent during build-out
- You're not generating revenue while you build; landlord should carry you
- Negotiate: "3 months free rent during construction, starting from lease execution"

**Lease Term**
- 5 years minimum (to amortize your build-out)
- 10 years with 2× 5-year renewal options is ideal for a strong location
- Shorter term = more risk that landlord won't renew when you're profitable

**Personal Guarantee**
- Landlord will want your personal guarantee on the lease
- Negotiate: Limited guarantee (first 12–24 months) or a "good guy clause" (if you give 3 months notice and surrender in good condition, guarantee ends)
- Never personally guarantee more than you can afford to lose

**Assignment & Subletting Rights**
- You need the right to assign the lease if you sell the restaurant or need to exit
- Without this, the lease is worthless to a buyer and you're trapped

**Exclusivity Clause**
- If in a multi-tenant building, negotiate that the landlord won't rent to a direct competitor (same cuisine type) in the same building
- Especially important in strip malls and food halls

**CAM Cap**
- Negotiate a maximum annual increase on CAM charges (3–5% cap per year)
- Without a cap, CAM can spike significantly

**Rent Escalation**
- Standard: 3% per year
- Try to negotiate: CPI-tied increases (often lower than 3%) or flat for first 2 years

**Build-Out Approval**
- Lease should give you right to make improvements with landlord consent (not unreasonably withheld)
- Get the process clear in writing before signing

### What to Have Reviewed
Hire a commercial real estate attorney to review before signing. Cost: $500–$1,500 flat fee. It's the best investment you'll make in Stage 3. The attorney will catch clauses that could cost you tens of thousands.

## Letter of Intent (LOI) Process

Before the full lease, both parties sign a non-binding Letter of Intent. This is where you negotiate all major terms.

1. Tour space and indicate interest
2. Submit LOI with your desired terms
3. Landlord counters; negotiate (usually 2–4 rounds)
4. Agreed LOI → attorneys draft full lease
5. Both parties sign lease; pay security deposit

**Timeline:** LOI to signed lease typically takes 4–10 weeks.

**Security Deposit:** Usually 2–3 months of base rent. In San Jose at $4,000–$8,000/month base, expect $8,000–$24,000 due at signing.

## How to Find Spaces

- **LoopNet** — Most widely used commercial listing site in SJ
- **CoStar** — More comprehensive; ask your broker for access
- **CREXi** — Good for off-market deals
- **Local commercial real estate brokers** — Use a tenant's rep broker (they're paid by the landlord, so free to you); they'll find unlisted spaces and negotiate on your behalf
  - Recommended area firms: Colliers International SJ, Cushman & Wakefield SJ, Kidder Mathews
- **Drive the target neighborhoods** — Many vacancies are listed with on-site signage before they hit LoopNet
- **Restaurant supply auctions** — Sometimes the building follows the equipment; track AuctionZip and RestaurantEquipment.bid for SJ-area closures
