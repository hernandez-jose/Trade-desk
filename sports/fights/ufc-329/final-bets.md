### Bout 1 — Main Event | 170 lbs | 5 Rounds
## ✅ Max Holloway def. Conor McGregor

**Confidence: High (82%)**

**The case in full:**
- McGregor's team initially demanded a 3-round format — a tacit admission from his own camp that he cannot compete for 5 rounds *(Source: MMA Fighting camp reports, May 2026)*
- Holloway holds all-time UFC records for significant strikes (3,655+) and total strikes (3,907+) — a volume system that dismantles ring-rusty fighters *(Source: UFC Stats)*
- McGregor has not competed since July 2021 — the longest layoff of any main event fighter in recent UFC history; cleared from anti-doping suspension only March 2026 *(Source: USADA, Bloody Elbow)*
- Sharp money moved Holloway from -265 (FanDuel open) to -550/-700 within hours of announcement — one of the sharpest initial line moves in recent UFC history *(Source: DraftKings Network, Covers)*
- McGregor publicly acknowledged the 2021 leg break "can still affect your head" — a pivot-dependent southpaw admitting psychological damage to his mechanical foundation *(Source: BJPenn/MMA Mania press conference coverage)*
- Holloway is 1-3 against southpaws in his career — the one honest vulnerability — but McGregor at 37, 5 years removed, cannot exploit this the way Poirier or a prime McGregor could *(Source: Sherdog head-to-head records)*

**Method:** Holloway TKO rounds 3–5 (volume accumulation, referee stoppage) or UD

### Bout 2 — Co-Main Event | 155 lbs | 3 Rounds
## ✅ Benoît Saint Denis def. Paddy Pimblett

**Confidence: Medium (62%)**

**The case in full:**
- BSD has finished his last 4 opponents, including a 16-second KO of Beneil Dariush — 6th fastest KO in UFC lightweight history *(Source: UFC Stats, MMA Fighting)*
- Under coach Nicolas Ott (hired late 2024), BSD implemented a data-driven training methodology with video-analyzed sparring and specialist clinch integration — his most significant structural upgrade *(Source: combatsmma.fr, Bloody Elbow)*
- Pimblett absorbed 144 significant strikes vs. Gaethje — more than his previous 7 UFC fights combined — while attempting only 5 takedowns in 25 minutes *(Source: UFC Stats via MiddleEasy)*
- Pimblett's 48% striking defense is one of the lowest among ranked UFC lightweights; BSD's southpaw left hand replicates the exact overhand right angle Gaethje used to repeatedly hurt Pimblett *(Source: UFC Stats, ESPN film analysis)*
- Pimblett walks around at 191 lbs and cuts to 155 — a 12+ lb water cut in under 24 hours confirmed at UFC 324 *(Source: Bloody Elbow, MiddleEasy weigh-in coverage)*
- BSD's 82% takedown defense neutralizes Pimblett's primary weapon

**Method:** BSD by TKO (striking accumulation) or Decision

### Bout 3 | 135 lbs | 3 Rounds
## ✅ Cory Sandhagen def. Mario Bautista

**Confidence: Low-Medium (55%)**

**The case in full:**
- 3-round format is the decisive variable: every one of Sandhagen's grappling losses (Sterling, Nurmagomedov, Dvalishvili) was in a 5-round fight. Bautista has 15 minutes, not 25, to impose wrestling dominance — a meaningful constraint on the grappling blueprint *(Source: UFC Stats, Sherdog)*
- Sandhagen's flying knee KO potential is the single highest-variance weapon on this fight card — it can end the fight in 3 seconds *(Source: UFC Stats — Frankie Edgar finish, 0:28 R1)*
- Sandhagen is under Trevor Wittman (8+ months, since UFC 320 loss) — the coach who rebuilt Rose Namajunas's camp specifically around grappling counters vs. Andrade *(Source: MMA Fighting coaching profiles)*
- Bautista averages 2.24 TD/15 min — significantly lower than Dvalishvili's relentless pace. He is a submission grappler, not a chain wrestler. The paths are different *(Source: UFC Stats)*

**⚠️ DISSENT NOTE — This is the most contested pick on the card:**
Bautista at +170 carries genuine +EV. True probability is closer to 50/50 than the -200 line implies. The 2019 armbar was on one week's notice in Bautista's UFC debut — analytically unreliable data. Sandhagen is 0–3 against elite grapplers as a structural ceiling. **If betting this fight, Bautista ML at +170 is the value side.** The "Sandhagen wins" verdict is marginal and reflects the 3-round format advantage only.

**Method:** Sandhagen KO/TKO (flying knee or head kick, early rounds)

### Bout 4 | 265 lbs | 3 Rounds
## ✅ Gable Steveson def. Elisha Ellison

**Confidence: High (80%)**

**The case in full:**
- Steveson's coaching staff — Greg Jackson, Mike Winkeljohn, Brandon Gibson, and Jon Jones daily — is the best any UFC debuting fighter has assembled *(Source: MMA Fighting camp coverage)*
- Jon Jones specifically designed a Dirty Boxing (DBX) competition for Steveson to develop his hands — explicit striking-first camp investment *(Source: Bloody Elbow, May 2026)*
- Steveson walks to the cage at 249 lbs naturally — no cut to 265, full weight and power *(Source: MFL 3 weigh-in records)*
- Ellison's only UFC appearance ended in a KO at 1:55, requiring oxygen and hospital transport — confirmed chin vulnerability *(Source: UFC Perth post-fight medical reports)*
- Ellison has zero documented takedown defense data against any quality wrestler — the central question of this fight is entirely unknown *(Source: Tapology, Sherdog career stats)*
- Curtis Blaydes (top-10 UFC HW) noted publicly: "He needs some work. He still needs to learn how to strike" — a credible heavyweight insider flag on Steveson's standup *(Source: MMA Junkie)*

**⚠️ BETTING NOTE — The -1800 line kills all ML value:**
Steveson wins this fight. He does NOT cover at -1800. The only rational bet is **Ellison ML at +900 at 0.5 units** as a pure +EV underdog play. Debut fighter trap + unknown chin from Steveson + Ellison's power = genuine upset scenario at live prices.

**Method:** Steveson TKO via ground-and-pound, rounds 1–2
