## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR Daukaus:** Elite submission specialist ("The D'Arce Knight," 6+ D'Arce finishes) on a 6-fight overall winning streak, including two sub-minute UFC finishes since returning in 2025 (43s KO of Michel Pereira; 50s D'Arce of Gerald Meerschaert). Strong takedown defense (83%) makes him hard to simply hold down, and his front-headlock game punishes loose scrambles. If Nickal over-commits to a guillotine or gets reversed, Daukaus finishes fast.

**AGAINST Daukaus:** He needs the opponent to engage his grappling, and Nickal dictates where fights happen. Daukaus's offensive takedowns are weak (~24% accuracy), so he can't force the fight to the mat against a 3× NCAA champion. His striking defense is porous (43%, 3.58 SApM) and both UFC losses are KO/TKO (Dolidze — facial fractures; Anders) — a real durability/chin concern against a power-shot finisher. His UFC body of work is gatekeeper-level (4–4, 1 NC).

**Most likely path:** Daukaus loses a control-based decision or gets out-positioned and finished — UNLESS he catches Nickal in a transition for a D'Arce, which is his single realistic route to the upset.

## 3. Opponent Context
Bo Nickal (8–1) is the exact stylistic problem for Daukaus: a generational wrestler (11.04 TD/15min) who controls phase, plus one-shot power (Vieira head-kick KO) and 62% striking accuracy. Nickal can keep the fight standing to exploit Daukaus's 43% striking defense, OR take him down and pin him while *avoiding* the front-headlock exchanges Daukaus thrives in. Daukaus can't out-wrestle him and is unlikely to out-strike him cleanly. The only door Nickal leaves open is a self-inflicted scramble error — which is precisely the door Daukaus is elite at walking through.

## 4. Striking Analysis
### Offense
Functional southpaw output, 3.04 SLpM, ~50% accuracy. Showed real power in the 43-second overhand-right KO of Pereira, but he is not a high-level technical striker.

### Defense
The glaring weakness: **43% striking defense, 3.58 SApM** — he absorbs a lot. Both UFC losses are KO/TKO; the Dolidze loss caused multiple facial fractures requiring surgical plates. Against a precise power-puncher, this is a live KO risk.

### Historical Striking Data
SLpM 3.04 / accuracy ~50% / SApM 3.58 / striking defense 43% / knockdown avg 0.47 (UFCStats/CBS). The SApM and defense numbers are the red flags.

## 5. Grappling Analysis
### Offensive Wrestling
Weak as a phase-forcing tool — ~24% TD accuracy (9/38), 2.1 TD/15min. He does not shoot and finish takedowns reliably, especially against a wrestler of Nickal's caliber. He needs the fight to come to him.

### Defensive Wrestling
Genuinely strong — **83% takedown defense**. Long limbs, good hips, fights back to scrambles. This is what keeps him competitive: he may not be planted on his back easily, which keeps the scramble (and the D'Arce) in play.

### Scrambles & Submissions
Elite. 1.64 sub/15min, 6+ career D'Arce finishes, fast (50s on Meerschaert). The front-headlock/D'Arce off a sprawl or a scramble is his money technique and the entire basis of his upset path.

### Cage Wrestling
Long frame makes him awkward to clinch against; competent fighting hands off the fence. But he can't pin Nickal there — he wins clinch exchanges via choke entries, not control.

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: D'Arce / front-headlock submission off a scramble (~70% of his win equity)
Nickal over-commits to a takedown or guillotine, Daukaus catches the neck, finishes fast. His only high-probability winning route.

### Path 2: Counter-KO in a striking exchange (~20%)
Nickal stands and gets careless; Daukaus lands the overhand (Pereira-style). Lower probability vs. Nickal's accuracy and power edge.

### Path 3: Grind-out decision (~10%)
Stuffs takedowns all night (83% TDD), out-lands on the feet, steals rounds. Unlikely given the athleticism gap and his striking defense.
