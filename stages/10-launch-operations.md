# Stage 10: Soft Open → Scale

**Timeline:** Months 10–12+ (ongoing)
**Goal:** Open successfully, stabilize operations, hit break-even, then build toward scale.

---

## The First 90 Days: Survival Mode

Most restaurants that fail do so in the first 12 months. The first 90 days are when you identify and fix the structural problems — in your kitchen, your staffing, your menu, and your cost model — before they become existential. Treat this phase as an extended beta test, not a finished product.

**What success looks like at Day 90:**
- Food cost holding at or below target (≤32%)
- Labor cost trending toward target (≤35%)
- Yelp/Google at 4.0+ with 20+ reviews
- At least one recurring corporate catering account
- Delivery platform orders growing week-over-week
- Staff turnover under 25% in the first 90 days
- You (the owner) are sleeping at least 6 hours a night

## Soft Open Protocol

### Why a Soft Open (Not a Grand Opening First)
A soft open lets you discover your operational failures in a controlled environment before you're reviewed publicly and before your reputation is set. The first 50 customers you serve set the tone for the next 50,000.

### Soft Open Structure
**Week 1 (Invite only — friends, family, staff friends):**
- Service at 50% capacity
- Run every item on the menu at least twice
- Time every ticket; log every mistake
- End of each service: full team debrief (15 minutes) — what broke, what worked

**Week 2 (Semi-public — invite neighbors, local businesses, influencers):**
- Increase to 75% capacity
- Offer a modest discount (20% off) as a thank-you and to set expectations
- Actively collect feedback: table cards, QR code to a Google Form
- Identify the 2–3 biggest operational bottlenecks and fix them before Week 3

**Week 3 (Public soft open):**
- Full service at full price
- Announce on social media; drive email list to come in
- Monitor every review in real time

**Hard open announcement:** After 3 weeks of stable service.

## Daily Operations System

### Opening Checklist (AI-Generated, Trainual-Hosted)
**BOH (Back of House) — 2 hours before service:**
- [ ] Receive and inspect any AM deliveries; check temperatures; log
- [ ] Prep lists completed and checked off
- [ ] All equipment turned on and verified at correct temperature
- [ ] Walk-in and reach-in temps logged (must be ≤41°F; required documentation)
- [ ] Hood filters checked and clean
- [ ] All prep containers labeled with date and initials (California law)
- [ ] Station mise en place complete and signed off by lead cook

**FOH (Front of House) — 30 minutes before service:**
- [ ] Tables set, menus stocked
- [ ] POS system opened and tested
- [ ] Online ordering and delivery platform status verified (accepting orders?)
- [ ] Staff briefed on 86'd items, specials, and any known issues
- [ ] Restrooms checked (clean, stocked)

### Closing Checklist (BOH)
- [ ] All food properly stored, labeled, and dated
- [ ] All temperatures documented
- [ ] All surfaces cleaned and sanitized per HACCP plan
- [ ] Grease traps checked (if floor trap)
- [ ] Trash emptied and secured
- [ ] Dish machine cleaned and drained
- [ ] Walk-in and freezer temps logged before leaving
- [ ] Alarm set; last person out documented

### Shift Change Protocol
- Outgoing lead briefs incoming lead verbally AND in writing (daily log book or a Slack/text channel)
- Issues from prior service flagged before new service begins
- Cash counted and reconciled at every shift change

## The First Cash Flow Crisis (Plan for It)

Even with a solid plan, most restaurants hit a cash crunch in months 2–4. Revenue is growing but not yet at break-even; costs are fixed. Plan:

1. **Working capital reserve:** Never open with less than 3 months of fixed costs in the bank
2. **Line of credit:** Apply before you need it. Toast Capital, Bluevine, or your SBA lender can set up a business line of credit
3. **Adjust faster:** If food cost runs 3% over target for 2 consecutive weeks — find the cause and fix it. Don't wait.
4. **Trim before you cut:** Reduce hours before you lay off a good employee; reduce menu before you raise prices dramatically

## Month 3–6: Optimization Phase

By Month 3 you have real data. Now is when AI earns its keep.

### Menu Optimization
Run the first full menu engineering matrix with real sales data:
- Which items are Dogs? Remove them by Month 4.
- Which are Puzzles? Reposition with photos, descriptions, or server recommendations.
- Which Stars need better photography or placement on the menu/digital board?

### Labor Optimization
Use 7shifts to analyze:
- Which shifts are consistently overstaffed?
- What's the actual revenue-per-labor-hour by daypart?
- Where does overtime accumulate?

Typically: Tuesday and Wednesday lunches are the first cut; Sunday brunch is the first add.

### Delivery Channel Mix
By Month 3 you'll know your delivery platform economics:
- DoorDash at 28% commission: what's your net margin per order after platform fee + food cost?
- Direct ordering via Toast at 2–3% fee: the same order at 25% higher margin
- Calculate: how many customers would you need to convert to direct ordering per month to save $X?
- Build a direct ordering incentive: "Order on our website, get [free drink / 10% off]"

### Corporate Catering Growth
By Month 3 you should have 1–2 corporate accounts. Target 5 by Month 6. Each account = $2,000–$8,000/month in high-margin revenue.

**System:** After each catering event:
1. AI sends a follow-up email 48 hours later: "Thank you — here's the invoice and our next month's calendar"
2. 2-week check-in email: "Are you planning any events in the next 30 days?"
3. Monthly newsletter to catering clients: seasonal menu changes, new offerings

## Month 6–12: Scale Decisions

If the core model is working (prime cost ≤65%, growing revenue, stable team), you face scale decisions:

### Option 1: Add a Delivery-Only Virtual Brand
From your existing kitchen, launch a second "brand" with a different menu and different DoorDash/Uber Eats listing — no additional rent, minimal additional labor.

Example: Your Korean fast casual already runs. Launch "South Bay Ramen" as a ghost brand from the same kitchen. Different product, different customer, same kitchen team. Revenue can increase 20–40% with minimal cost.

### Option 2: Expand Operating Hours
If you're currently lunch-only, model the incremental revenue and cost of adding dinner service. In San Jose, dinner hours typically yield higher average checks.

### Option 3: Second Location
Criteria for a second location:
- First location has been profitable for ≥6 months
- Operations can run without owner present (i.e., you have a manager or GM)
- Concept is fully systematized in Trainual and can be replicated
- Cash or financing in place before signing a second lease

**Do not sign a second lease if the first location isn't operationally stable.** The most common cause of multi-location failure is expanding too soon.

### Option 4: Franchise or Licensing
Longer-term (3–5 years) if your concept has proven replicability, brand recognition, and systemized operations. Not a Year 1 consideration.

## Emergency Protocols

### If You're Getting Crushed With Orders (Good Problem)
- Drop slow-selling menu items from the delivery menu same-day
- Increase ticket minimums on delivery platforms
- Put platforms on "pause" if kitchen is backed up more than 30 minutes
- Do not sacrifice quality for speed — one viral negative review is worth months of bad PR

### If Food Cost Spikes
- Pull MarketMan report: which items drove the spike?
- Check for theft (compare theoretical vs. actual for every item)
- Check portion drift (are line cooks eyeballing instead of using portioning tools?)
- Check vendor invoices against purchase orders (price increases?)
- Do not wait — food cost recovery takes 2–3 weeks of discipline

### If a Key Employee Quits
- Cross-train from day one: every cook should know 2 stations
- Have a 2-week "emergency staffing" protocol: owner covers, hours may temporarily reduce
- Gusto + Indeed integrations allow fast job posting within hours

### If You Get a Bad Health Inspection
- Address every citation before the re-inspection
- Do not argue with the inspector — ever
- Fix systemic issues, not just the item cited
- Post your score card (required in CA) — a 90+ "A" is expected; a B or C needs explanation
