# Josh Hokit — Deep Fight Research
## UFC Freedom 250 | vs. Derrick Lewis | June 14, 2026 | 265 lbs (Heavyweight) | 3 Rounds

> Analyst note: Hokit is the younger, fresher, far higher-pace athlete with a real wrestling base against a 41-year-old, one-shot KO artist whose cardio and takedown defense are shot. The only thing he has to respect is Lewis's Round-1 power. Manage the first five minutes, then it should be a route to a finish or a comfortable decision.
> Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR Hokit:** He is 28, undefeated (9-0, 3-0 UFC), a former D1 All-American wrestler (peaked #3 nationally) and elite athlete who already beat #7-ranked Curtis Blaydes over three rounds in a Fight-of-the-Year contender (UFC 327, UD) — a far better opponent than Lewis is today. He out-paces and can out-wrestle Lewis, whose 53% takedown defense and notoriously poor cardio are tailor-made to be exploited by a high-output pressure wrestler. **AGAINST Hokit:** He is undersized for heavyweight (6'1"/73" reach vs. Lewis's 6'3"/79"), defensively porous (44% striking defense, 6.81 SApM), and at heavyweight any defensive lapse against the all-time UFC KO leader can end the night in one punch — especially in a stand-and-trade Round 1. **Most likely path:** Hokit pressures, mixes in wrestling, weathers/avoids the early power, and finishes a fading Lewis in Round 2 or wins a clear decision.

## 3. Opponent Context
Lewis threatens exactly one thing: a one-shot knockout, most likely in Round 1 via the overhand right/uppercut. Everything else favors Hokit. Lewis's 53% takedown defense invites Hokit's wrestling; Lewis's poor cardio means the longer it goes, the worse it gets for him; Lewis's low output (2.43 SLpM) means he can be out-worked on the feet too. The danger is that Hokit's own 44% striking defense + willingness to trade hands gives Lewis the firefight opening he needs early. Hokit's discipline in the first five minutes is the whole fight.

## 4. Striking Analysis
### Offense
Heavy, accurate power with high output for the division (UFC sample: 9.25 SLpM, 62% accuracy — inflated by short finishes and the Blaydes slugfest, but directionally elite). Comes forward, lands first, fights in the pocket. Two R1 TKOs show one-punch capability of his own.
### Defense
The clear hole: 44% striking defense, 6.81 SApM. He eats a lot of return fire and is willing to trade — acceptable vs. most, dangerous vs. the all-time KO leader.
### Historical Striking Data
SLpM 9.25 / Acc 62% / SApM 6.81 / Def 44% (UFC, 3-fight sample — treat as noisy). *(Source: aggregated UFCStats via [CBS Sports fighter page](https://www.cbssports.com/ufc/fighter/2257897/josh-hokit/) reporting; verify on UFCStats.)*

## 5. Grappling Analysis

3 career submissions; opportunistic on the mat. Against Lewis, top control + ground-and-pound is a higher-percentage finish than hunting a sub.

Athletic and strong; can chain takedowns and pin a tiring Lewis against the fence to drain him and rack up control time.

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: Pace-and-pressure finish, R2 (most likely, ~40%)
Survive/avoid Lewis's R1 power, push the pace, mix in takedowns; Lewis fades and Hokit finishes with strikes in Round 2 (or late R1 after Lewis empties the tank).
### Path 2: Clear decision (~35%)
If Lewis stays upright and defensive, Hokit out-works and out-wrestles him for 15 minutes — exactly what he did to a tougher Blaydes.
### Path 3: Wrestling-dominant grind / GnP TKO (~10–15%)
Lewis's 53% TDD lets Hokit take him down repeatedly, control, and damage him on the mat.

## 12. X-Factors
- **Hokit's discipline in Round 1** — the single biggest variable; if he brawls recklessly, Lewis's power is live.
- **Quick turnaround / Blaydes-war damage** — ~2 months after a brutal three-rounder; any lingering damage could matter.
- **Big-stage spotlight** — White House / 250th-anniversary spectacle; emotion could push Hokit to seek a highlight finish (slightly raises early-trade risk).
- **Heavyweight variance** — one clean Lewis shot ends it regardless of the gap.

## 13. Risk Factors & Confidence Assessment
### Primary Risks to Hokit Winning
1. Eats a clean Lewis bomb in Round 1 while trading (his 44% defense + Lewis's all-time power).
2. Gets too aggressive early and gives Lewis the firefight he needs.
3. Undersized vs. a bigger man — gets bullied in close if he can't impose wrestling.
### Confidence Level
**Model win estimate: ~79–80% for Hokit** — capped below the model's 88% ceiling because of (a) heavyweight one-punch variance, (b) Hokit's leaky defense vs. the all-time KO leader, and (c) the small elite-level sample. This is meaningfully above his implied ~80–82%, so the straight ML is roughly fair-to-slightly-negative value. The edge, if any, is in props (round/method), not the moneyline.
