## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR Magomedov:** 10-0 with every win a finish, evenly split between knockouts and submissions — and his opponent (Baghdasaryan) has two career submission losses, 0% takedown offense, and ~57% takedown defense. Younger (26 vs. 34), fresher (Baghdasaryan is off a ~16-month layoff and a R1 KO loss), and he can win this fight where Baghdasaryan is weakest: on the mat. He even carries R1 KO power (DWCS contract win), so the standing exchange is not safe for the veteran either. **AGAINST Magomedov:** Zero UFC tape; his 10-0 was built on the regional circuit and DWCS, a clear step below UFC featherweight. He has never been past the early rounds — cardio and composure in deep water are unknown — and he is walking into the Octagon for the first time against a K-1-caliber striker who can end it with one shot. At −290 the price already bakes in most of the upside. **Most likely path:** Takedown and submission (or ground-and-pound TKO), most likely R1–R2.

## 3. Opponent Context
Baghdasaryan is the ideal first UFC opponent for a grappler: a pure striker with no takedown offense, mediocre takedown defense (~57%), and a documented pattern of being submitted once grounded. Magomedov does not need to win a kickboxing match; he needs one or two clean entries to the mat. The only opponent threat is Baghdasaryan's southpaw KO power early — Magomedov must respect range before closing distance.
