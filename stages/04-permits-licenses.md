# Stage 4: Permits & Licenses

**Timeline:** Months 4–7 (overlaps with build-out; start immediately after lease signing)
**Goal:** Obtain every required permit and license before opening day. Missing one can shut you down.

> **San Jose Reality Check:** Permitting in San Jose is slower than most California cities. Budget 3–6 months for the full permit stack. Start immediately — do not wait for build-out to finish before filing.

---

## Complete Permit & License Checklist

### City of San Jose Permits

**Business License**
- Issuing agency: City of San Jose, Office of the City Clerk
- Cost: ~$125–$500/year (based on gross receipts)
- Timeline: 2–5 business days online
- File at: sanjoseca.gov/business
- Renew annually

**Building Permit (for Tenant Improvements)**
- Required for any construction, electrical, plumbing, or HVAC work
- Issuing agency: City of San Jose, Department of Planning, Building and Code Enforcement
- Cost: Based on valuation of work — budget 1–3% of construction costs ($1,500–$15,000+)
- Timeline: 4–12 weeks for plan check; expedited review available for additional fee
- Process: Submit plans (drawn by licensed architect or engineer) → plan check → corrections → permit issued → inspections during build-out → final sign-off
- File at: sanjoseca.gov/permits

**Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)**
- Issued after final building inspection confirms the space is safe for occupancy
- Required before you can open to the public
- Timeline: 1–3 weeks after final inspection

**Sign Permit**
- Required for any exterior signage
- Issuing agency: City of San Jose Planning
- Cost: $200–$800
- Timeline: 2–6 weeks

**Encroachment Permit (if using sidewalk/patio)**
- Required for any outdoor dining on public sidewalk
- Issuing agency: City of San Jose Department of Public Works
- Cost: $500–$2,000/year
- Timeline: 4–8 weeks

### Santa Clara County Permits

**Environmental Health Permit (Health Permit)**
- Issuing agency: Santa Clara County Environmental Health
- This is the most critical permit — you cannot open without it
- Cost: $1,500–$3,000/year (based on restaurant type and seating capacity)
- Timeline: 4–8 weeks after construction is complete
- Process:
  1. Pre-application meeting (free; strongly recommended)
  2. Submit plan check ($500–$1,200 fee) before build-out begins
  3. Build out to approved plans
  4. Final inspection by Environmental Health inspector
  5. Permit issued
- Key requirements:
  - 3-compartment sink
  - Handwashing sinks (separate from prep sinks, in required locations)
  - Proper ventilation / Type 1 hood with fire suppression
  - NSF-certified commercial equipment
  - Food storage off the floor
  - Refrigeration capable of maintaining 41°F or below
  - Hot holding at 135°F or above
- File at: sccgov.org/sites/eh

**Hazardous Materials Business Plan (HMBP)**
- Required if you store cleaning chemicals above certain thresholds
- Usually minimal for small restaurants
- File through CUPA (Certified Unified Program Agency) — same as Environmental Health in Santa Clara County

### California State Permits

**Seller's Permit (Sales Tax Permit)**
- Issuing agency: California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA)
- Cost: Free
- Required to collect and remit California sales tax on food and beverages
- File at: cdtfa.ca.gov
- Timeline: Immediate (online)

**Employer Identification Number (EIN)**
- Federal; IRS.gov
- Free; immediate online
- Required for payroll and business banking

**Workers' Compensation Insurance**
- Required by California law from your first employee
- Cost: $3,000–$8,000/year for a small restaurant (varies by payroll size and claims history)
- Get quotes from: State Fund (SCIF), Employers Holdings, or your business insurance broker

**CDPH Food Facility License** (if applicable)
- For certain food manufacturing or processing — most restaurants don't need this separately; health permit covers it

### Alcohol Licenses (If Serving Alcohol)

California ABC licenses are complex, expensive, and slow. Plan for 3–6 months from application to approval.

| License Type | What It Allows | Application Cost | Annual Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type 41 (Beer & Wine) | Beer and wine with food | $300–$400 | $200–$300 | Faster approval than Type 47 |
| Type 47 (Full Liquor) | Full bar with food | $12,000–$20,000 | $900–$1,300 | Most common for full-service; market rate for license purchase $25K–$100K+ |
| Type 58 (Caterer's) | Off-premises catering | ~$500 | ~$200 | Required for catering alcohol at events |
| Type 68 (Instructional) | Cooking classes with tasting | Varies | Varies | Niche use case |

**Type 47 Reality in San Jose:**
- ABC quotas limit new licenses by population; San Jose has available quota, but a new license takes 90–120 days
- Alternatively, purchase an existing license from a closing restaurant — costs $25K–$80K but much faster
- Work with an ABC license consultant (fees: $1,500–$3,000) — they navigate the process and reduce rejection risk

**RBS (Responsible Beverage Service) Training:**
- California requires all alcohol servers to complete RBS training by Jan 1, 2022 (already in effect)
- $10–$30 per employee for online certification
- Track certificates — Health/ABC can request proof

### Food Handler & Food Safety Certifications

**Food Handler Cards**
- All food handlers must have a valid CA Food Handler Card within 30 days of hire
- Cost: $15–$25 per employee
- Providers: StateFoodSafety.com, ServSafe, Learn2Serve
- Valid for 3 years

**Food Safety Manager Certification (ServSafe)**
- At least one certified manager must be on-site during all operating hours
- Cost: ~$150–$200 for exam + materials
- Valid for 5 years
- Exam offered by ServSafe, Prometric

**HACCP Plan**
- Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points — food safety management system
- Not always required by Santa Clara County but strongly recommended
- Use Claude to draft your HACCP plan from a template; reduces foodborne illness risk and liability

## Permit Process Timeline

```
Month 4 (lease signed):
├── File Business License
├── Apply for Seller's Permit (CDTFA)
├── Schedule pre-application meeting with Santa Clara Environmental Health
├── Engage architect for building permit drawings
└── Start ABC license application if serving alcohol

Month 5:
├── Submit building permit plans to City of San Jose
├── Submit health permit plan check to Environmental Health
├── File workers' comp insurance
└── Set up EIN + payroll system

Months 5–7 (build-out):
├── Building permit issued → construction begins
├── Inspections during construction (framing, rough electrical, rough plumbing)
├── Final building inspection → Certificate of Occupancy
└── Environmental Health final inspection

Month 7 (pre-opening):
├── All staff complete Food Handler cards
├── Manager completes ServSafe certification
├── Health permit issued
├── Business license posted
└── ABC license issued (if started Month 4)
```

## Common Permit Mistakes That Delay Opening

1. **Starting construction before permits** — City can issue stop-work orders; restores to pre-work state at your cost
2. **Not doing a pre-application meeting with Environmental Health** — Changes required after plan check can add 4–8 weeks
3. **Using residential-grade equipment** — Must be NSF-certified commercial; Environmental Health will flag this
4. **Missing handwashing sink locations** — Must be within the required distance of all food prep areas; cannot be a prep sink
5. **Incorrect grease trap sizing** — Must be sized to your kitchen's output; undersized traps are a violation
6. **Not getting sign permits** — City can make you remove signage that cost $10,000
