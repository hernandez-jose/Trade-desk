# Hyder Amil — Deep Fight Research
## UFC Fight Night: Kape vs. Horiguchi (UFC Vegas 119) | vs. Christian Rodriguez | June 20, 2026 | 145 lbs | 3 Rounds (main card)

> Analyst note: Amil is a dangerous high-volume, switch-stance striker with KO power and elite cardio — but his last two fights exposed the exact flaw Rodriguez is built to exploit: he can be taken down and controlled at will, and he can be cracked when he presses recklessly. To win, he must keep it standing and out-volume a low-output wrestler.
> Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR Amil:** Clearly the better, busier striker with one-shot power (6 of 11 wins by KO/TKO) and a relentless pace that can drown a low-volume opponent. Rodriguez strikes at only ~1.73 SLpM — if Amil keeps the fight vertical, he wins the striking battle and likely the rounds, with real KO upside.
**AGAINST Amil:** Takedown defense is a glaring, twice-relevant hole — Jamall Emmers controlled him 8:35 on 7 takedowns just to win a decision. Rodriguez is a better wrestler than Emmers with a live submission game (4 career subs). Amil also has no offensive grappling to threaten back, and was KO'd in 26 seconds by Delgado, showing the chin/defense can be cracked. He is 0–2 in his last two and fighting for his roster spot.
**Most likely path:** Amil needs early stuffs and a KO/volume-decision standing. The more probable script is Rodriguez putting him on his back and controlling — Amil's path is real but the lower-probability one.

*(Sources: Cageside Press, LowKickMMA, KTVU, UFC.com, Sherdog, Tapology, Wikipedia)*

## 3. Opponent Context
Rodriguez (12–4) is a Roufusport wrestler-grappler: ~100% cited TD accuracy, ~3.0 sub avg, 4 career submissions, but low striking volume (~1.73 SLpM) and shaky TD defense (~43%). Crucially, Rodriguez's own TD-defense flaw is irrelevant because Amil doesn't shoot — meaning the grappling exchanges all run in Rodriguez's favor. Rodriguez's low output is Amil's opening: if Amil can stay upright, he out-strikes him comfortably.

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: Keep it standing, win on volume/power (his best path)
Stuff early takedowns, out-strike a low-output opponent, bank rounds or land the KO. Requires major TD-defense improvement.
### Path 2: Early KO (live secondary)
Catch Rodriguez pressing or in a striking exchange — Amil has the power and Rodriguez's striking defense (~49%) is hittable.
### Path 3: Survive grappling, steal late rounds (low probability)
Get up repeatedly and out-work in the standing windows — hard to do given his wall-walking limitations.

## 12. X-Factors
- **Takedown defense improvement** — has he fixed the Emmers/Delgado-era hole? If yes, he's live; if not, he loses the same way.
- **Power equalizer** — one clean shot changes everything; Rodriguez has been submitted but not often badly hurt standing.
- **Apex small cage** aids his pressure but also makes it easier to be pinned to the fence and grounded — net wash.

3. Skid/desperation could lead to over-aggression and a counter (the Delgado pattern).

Amil's path requires keeping a fight standing against a wrestler whose entire game is to prevent that, and whose TD-defense flaw is neutralized because Amil doesn't shoot. **Model estimate: ~38% Amil.** Against an implied ~43.5–44.4%, Amil is slightly OVER-priced as a dog — no value on his side. He is the side to fade here, but only mildly; this is not a strong-conviction spot either way.
