## Method of Victory Prediction

**Predicted Winner: Cory Sandhagen**
**Method:** Decision (Unanimous or Split)
**Confidence:** Moderate (58–60%)
**Round:** Full 3 rounds

**Reasoning:**
The CES framework gives Sandhagen a narrow edge (+0.40) driven by superior striking quality, physical attributes (reach/height), and deeper high-stakes experience. This is not a mismatch — it is a competitive contender fight where Sandhagen's striking repertoire and reach advantage tip the scales. Bautista is significantly more dangerous than he was in 2019 (UFC debut), having compiled an 8-fight win streak and genuine submission finishing ability. However, Sandhagen is unlikely to be caught in the same submission position twice; he submitted Bautista from a dominant striking entry in 2019 and will be more cautious on the mat.

The most likely path to a Bautista upset: he closes distance early, disrupts Sandhagen's range management, and finds a rear-naked choke or guillotine in a grinding second or third round. Bautista's switch stance and elevated wrestling may give him more success than in 2019.

Sandhagen's most likely path: counter-striking off Bautista's aggressive pressure, controlling distance with the jab/front kick, landing a clean knee or spinning attack in R2 or R3. A finish is possible but Bautista has not been stopped in his UFC run. Decision is the likely endpoint.

## Final Verdict

> **Analytics verdict: Cory Sandhagen, Decision, ~58–60% win probability**
>
> The CES framework produces a narrow differential of +0.40 in Sandhagen's favor, mapping to a 58–60% probability of victory — a coin flip with a modest edge. Sandhagen's dominance in the striking domain (8.2 vs. 6.8), physical attributes (71" reach, 5'11"), and experience bank are the deciding factors. Bautista is a genuinely dangerous opponent — more evolved than in 2019, with submission finishing power and switch-stance striking angles that did not exist in their first meeting. This rematch is competitive enough that a Bautista upset would not be an upset in the traditional sense. The CES framework does not see a KO/TKO finish as the most likely result for either fighter (Sandhagen has never been KO'd; Bautista has never been stopped in the UFC). Expect a closely contested 3-round decision with Sandhagen landing the cleaner, more technical shots en route to a narrow win on two or three judges' cards.
