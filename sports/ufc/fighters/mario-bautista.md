## Fighting Style

**Versatile pressure grappler with active submission game and improving striking**

Bautista is one of the most well-rounded fighters in the UFC bantamweight division — a switch-stance hybrid who can hurt you standing, drag you to the mat, and submit you from multiple positions. He started as a high school wrestler in Winnemucca, Nevada, relocated to Arizona to train at MMA Lab under head coach John Crouch, and gradually built out a complete game. He holds a brown belt in Brazilian jiu-jitsu. His strengths are grinding grappling pressure and choking out opponents; his striking has improved significantly through his win streak.

### Grappling
Bautista built his career on wrestling and submission grappling. He uses clinch work and cage pressure to set up takedowns rather than explosive doubles or singles — he earns his takedowns with patience and body positioning. Once on top he is dangerous: he passes guard, takes the back, and hunts rear-naked chokes. His 0.8 submission attempts per 15 minutes ranks him among the more active finishers in the division. His takedown accuracy (33%) is modest — he does not shoot recklessly, but when he commits, he tends to use chains and trips rather than brute-force shots. His takedown defense (66%) is solid enough to prevent purely grappling-based opponents from simply turning the tables.

### Striking
Bautista is a switch-stance striker who has added real punch to his stand-up over his win streak. His SLpM of 5.33 is well above bantamweight average, and his 47% accuracy is respectable. He is not a power puncher in the traditional sense — his two striking finishes came from TKO follow-up after a flying knee (Johns) and a ground-and-pound sequence — but he works well at mid-range, mixes head and body attacks, and pressures opponents into the cage to set up grappling transitions. His strike absorption (3.91 SApM) and defense (54%) are acceptable but not elite; he can be hit by active strikers.

### Cardio
Bautista has solid cardio for three-round fights and has never been stopped by fatigue. He paces himself well, using the grappling game to control work rate. He has not yet been tested in a 5-round UFC main event, which is a relevant unknown heading into any championship or high-stakes bout.

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. Cory Sandhagen — UFC FN (January 2019, Loss, Armbar R1)
Bautista's UFC debut and a rough introduction. Sandhagen submitted him via armbar in 3:31 of Round 1, exposing submission defense gaps and the danger of fighting an elite technician on his debut. This loss defined the early chapter — and the upcoming rematch at UFC 329 frames it as one of the more compelling stories in the division.

### vs. Miles Johns — UFC 247 (February 2020, TKO R2)
Bautista's first signature win in the UFC, finishing Johns with a flying knee and follow-up punches in Round 2. Earned a Performance of the Night bonus. Demonstrated his athleticism, timing, and ability to produce highlight-reel finishes when given the right opponent.

### vs. Ricky Simon — UFC FN 234 (January 2024, UD W)
A gritty, closely contested fight that Bautista ground out against a fellow top-15 contender known for his own grappling. Winning ugly against Simon — who had upset multiple ranked fighters — proved Bautista belonged in the upper tier of the division.

### vs. José Aldo — UFC 307 (October 2024, Split Decision W)
One of the most significant wins of Bautista's career. Defeating a UFC Hall of Famer and former featherweight champion — even controversially — put Bautista firmly on the contender map. He used cage control and takedown pressure to neutralize Aldo's elite striking, winning two of three rounds on the majority of scorecards. The fight showed his gameplan discipline but also his limitations as a finisher (he never landed a takedown cleanly despite constant attempts).

### vs. Umar Nurmagomedov — UFC 321 (October 2025, UD Loss)
Bautista's most revealing fight. He ended an 8-fight win streak against the UFC's pound-for-pound grappling threat. He landed a clean flying knee that floored Umar in Round 2 — the best moment of his career — but Nurmagomedov's chain wrestling and relentless cage pressure neutralized him across 15 minutes. The unanimous 30-27 loss clarified the ceiling: Bautista is a legitimate top-5 contender, but against elite grapplers who can also strike, he gets outworked.

### Method of Victory Markets
- **Decision**: Highest probability outcome — roughly 58% of his UFC wins went to decision. Back decision props when available.
- **Submission**: Second most likely at ~36% of his UFC wins (4 of 11). If he gets top position or takes the back, the rear-naked choke is his primary finish. Submission props offer value at standard juice.
- **KO/TKO**: Low probability as the winner (~9% UFC win rate, 1 of 11). As a method for either fighter, it is not zero — his Trevin Jones loss and the Umar knockdown show both parties can get caught.

### Totals
- **Over 1.5 rounds**: Strong lean. Bautista has gone past Round 1 in 10 of 11 UFC fights (only loss to Sandhagen came in R1, as the loser).
- **Over 2.5 rounds**: Solid lean in most matchups — he grinds fights out and his submissions come in R2 or later.
- **Under 1.5**: Avoid unless the opponent is a massive KO artist. Bautista does not get finished early as a winner.
