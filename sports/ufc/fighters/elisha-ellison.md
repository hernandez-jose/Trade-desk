## Fighting Style

Ellison is a raw, pressure-forward heavyweight with exceptional knockout power and a developing grappling game. He relies almost exclusively on first-round aggression — every professional win has come via stoppage within the first two rounds. His BJJ blue belt underpins some guillotine-trapping ability up close, but his primary threat is his heavy hands in combination exchanges. He has shown willingness to wade forward and trade, accepting damage in pursuit of the finish.

The Pericic loss exposed the biggest question marks in his game: chin durability under quality punchers and the ability to survive adversity when hurt. He required an oxygen tank post-fight and was transported to hospital for CT scans, suggesting the KO was severe. His cardio was also self-identified as a weakness early in his career — he has acknowledged that a 2023 loss woke him up to the need for conditioning work.

### Grappling

Ellison holds a BJJ blue belt and has demonstrated front-headlock and guillotine choke awareness. He has one professional submission win (guillotine vs. Tuani, MFN 7). His grappling is competent at the regional level but untested against UFC-caliber wrestlers. Against Pericic, he pulled guard in desperation after being hurt standing — an instinctive move that backfired against a heavier, stronger finisher. Takedown defense is effectively unknown at the UFC level (0% in 1 bout, 1:55 long). He is likely to struggle against elite wrestling, particularly Gable Steveson-level shot-and-level-change specialists.

### Striking

Heavy-handed orthodox brawler with demonstrable KO power — four of five wins are stoppages via punches. He throws with volume and aggression and shows some combination work, but his technique is raw. He has a tendency to stand in front and exchange, leaving himself open to counters. Against Pericic, he was hurt by an overhand right counter after his own offensive burst. His 33% striking accuracy in his sole UFC appearance is partially an artifact of a chaotic short fight, but suggests some wildness in his punching. He does not appear to use his jab to control range effectively and tends to load up on bigger shots.

### Cardio

Self-acknowledged weak point early in career. Ellison has stated that a 2023 loss (Muckleshoot FN 6 or 9) revealed how poor his cardio was and prompted a training overhaul. All five professional wins came in Round 1 (or very early Round 2), meaning his cardio has never truly been tested in competition. This is a major red flag heading into the Steveson fight — if he cannot find a finish early, his conditioning under a wrestling-heavy grind remains a complete unknown at the UFC level.

## Career Defining Fights

**Loss vs. Brando Pericic — UFC Fight Night 260, Perth, Sep 27, 2025**
This was Ellison's UFC debut, taken on short notice after both fighters were pulled from Dana White's Contender Series and slotted onto the Perth card. Pericic opened with heavy leg kicks and immediately found his range. Ellison responded with an aggressive combination burst, but was caught by a crushing overhand right that sent him stumbling. Pericic swarmed, forcing Ellison to pull guard in desperation. Pericic unloaded brutal ground-and-pound from top position and stopped the fight at 1:55. Ellison was unconscious on the canvas, needed oxygen post-fight, and was transported to hospital for CT scans. The severity of the stoppage makes this more than a loss — it raises serious questions about his durability against UFC-level heavyweights.

**Win vs. Rafael Brewster — Muckleshoot Fight Night 12, Jan 25, 2025 (Main Event)**
Ellison's final regional fight before earning his UFC shot. Finished Brewster by TKO (punches) in R1 at 3:21 in a main event slot — his most high-profile regional outing. This win capped a three-fight KO streak and landed him on the DWCS radar.

**Loss vs. Dave Pearson — Muckleshoot Fight Night 9, May 25, 2024**
Ellison's first professional loss. Submitted via side choke in Round 2 at 0:45 — by far his longest fight at the time. By his own account, this loss exposed his conditioning and motivated a training overhaul. Context: Pearson is a regional fighter, so this was still a developmental setback rather than a quality-opponent loss.

### Method of Victory Markets
Ellison's 100% finish rate in wins means the over on fight duration and decision markets are weak bets when backing him. His best props market is **Ellison by KO/TKO in R1** at big underdog pricing — he carries legitimate one-punch power even against quality opponents. However, given the Pericic KO loss, the **opponent by KO/TKO** prop is often the better angle when Ellison is a dog — he has been stopped twice via strikes and appears hittable.

For the Steveson fight: **Steveson by submission or TKO** props are the primary value bets. Ellison has been submitted before and has no documented defense against Olympic-level wrestling. If Steveson can drag this to the mat, the finish probability is high.

### Totals
Ellison fights short. All 7 professional fights have ended in Round 1 or Round 2. The **under** is the correct bet in virtually every Ellison fight. When he's a live dog, a first-round action prop adds value. The **over 1.5 rounds** is a fade regardless of opponent.
