## Key Tactical Battles

### Battle 1 — Rangnick Gegenpressing vs. Messi Build-Up
Austria will press aggressively, trying to dispossess Argentina high up the pitch. Messi, however, is a master at evading pressure — his spatial intelligence means he almost never loses the ball under pressure. Julián Álvarez's movement also creates problems for Austrian center backs who don't know whether to track or stay. Advantage: **Argentina**.

### Battle 2 — Messi Free Kicks and Set Pieces
Austria conceded aerially vs Jordan. Messi's free kicks and Argentina's set-piece organization are a constant threat. Alaba is excellent but Austria's aerial defending as a unit is medium quality. **Argentine set pieces should yield at least one or two quality chances.**

### Battle 3 — Austrian Counter-Attack vs. Argentine High Line
If Austria win the ball in their own half, they break quickly with Arnautovic or Baumgartner. Argentina can be caught if their defensive line is too advanced. However, Scaloni has been meticulous about defensive organization. **Low risk for Argentina but not zero.**

## Injury & Suspension Impact

Argentina: Full squad available. Messi's hat trick in MD1 means he's in form and physically well. No concerns.

Austria: Arnautovic fitness is the key question. If he starts, Austria have a genuine aerial threat. If on the bench, Baumgartner starts as a shadow striker and Austria are less physical. The loss of Arnautovic reduces Austria's attacking quality from 7 to ~6.5.
