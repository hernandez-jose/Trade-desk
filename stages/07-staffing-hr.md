## California HR Reality

California is the most employee-protective state in the US. The PAGA law (Private Attorneys General Act) allows any employee to sue on behalf of all current and former employees for labor code violations — and attorneys take these cases on contingency. One missed meal break multiplied across 10 employees for 6 months is a six-figure liability. This is not hypothetical; it happens constantly to restaurant operators who copy their employment practices from other states.

**Budget for compliance from day one. It is cheaper than the litigation.**

## California Labor Law Essentials

### Wage Requirements (San Jose, 2025–2026)
- **State minimum wage:** $16.50/hour (California; verify current rate)
- **San Jose minimum wage:** Tracks state or higher — verify at sanjoseca.gov
- **Tip credit:** California has **no tip credit** — you must pay minimum wage regardless of tips
- **Overtime:** Time-and-a-half after **8 hours in a day** (not just 40 hours/week) — this is daily OT
- **Double time:** After 12 hours in a day; after 8 hours on the 7th consecutive workday

### Meal & Rest Breaks (Non-Negotiable)
- **30-minute unpaid meal break** before the end of the 5th hour of work
- If you fail to provide it: pay one extra hour of pay at regular rate (meal break premium)
- **10-minute paid rest break** per 4 hours worked (or major fraction thereof)
- If you fail to provide it: one extra hour at regular rate
- Document every break in your scheduling/time-tracking system — Gusto and 7shifts both have this built in

### Split Shift Premium
- If an employee works a split shift (e.g., lunch and dinner with a gap), they receive a premium of at least one hour at minimum wage above what they'd otherwise earn

### Required California Postings (Physical, in Workplace)
- CA Minimum Wage notice
- CA Paid Sick Leave notice
- Workers' Compensation notice
- Payday notice
- OSHA/Cal-OSHA notices
- Federal (FLSA, FMLA, EEOC, etc.)
- Poster compliance service ($30/mo via Poster Elite) manages this automatically

### Paid Sick Leave
- California requires at minimum **40 hours (5 days) of paid sick leave per year** (as of 2024)
- Accrues at 1 hour per 30 hours worked; front-loading alternative available
- Cannot be required to be used for anything other than qualifying reasons
- Gusto tracks and administers this automatically

## Recruiting in San Jose

### Where to Find Restaurant Staff
- **Indeed** — Highest volume for hourly roles; budget $200–$500/month for sponsored posts
- **Craigslist** — Still effective for cooks and hourly staff in the Bay Area
- **Culinary school partnerships** — San Jose City College, De Anza, City College of San Francisco have culinary programs; offer externship positions for free labor + pipeline
- **Employee referrals** — Your first hire is your best recruiter; build in referral bonuses ($200–$500 for a hire that lasts 90 days)
- **LinkedIn** — For managers and chefs
- **Restaurant networks** — San Jose restaurant community is small; word of mouth travels fast

### What Candidates in San Jose Expect
- Higher wages than national averages (plan for it)
- Clear scheduling (California's "fair scheduling" culture)
- Food handler card reimbursement
- Paid sick leave (legally required)
- No tip pooling without compliance (California allows tip pooling only among tip-eligible employees)
- Stable hours — gig economy sets a high bar for flexibility expectations

### Retention Factors (More Important Than Recruiting)
San Jose's hospitality labor market is competitive. Turnover in restaurants costs $3,000–$6,000 per employee when you factor in recruiting, onboarding, and the ramp period.

High-retention practices:
- Consistent scheduling posted at least 1 week in advance
- Staff meal / family meal daily
- Clear advancement path (prep → line → lead cook)
- Direct feedback culture — don't let problems fester
- Celebrate performance publicly; address problems privately
- Quarterly check-ins (use Trainual or Gusto performance tools)

Use Trainual to build your onboarding curriculum once, then every new hire goes through it on their phone:

### Week 1 Onboarding Modules (Build with Claude, Host on Trainual)
1. **Welcome & culture** — What we're building and why
2. **California safety & compliance** — Meal breaks, overtime, reporting injuries
3. **Food safety fundamentals** — Temperature control, cross-contamination, handwashing
4. **Your station orientation** — Equipment, location of everything, setup/breakdown
5. **Menu knowledge** — Every dish: ingredients, allergens, preparation method, portion size
6. **Customer interaction standards** — Greeting, handling complaints, upselling (if applicable)
7. **Opening and closing checklists** — Review and sign off

### Employee Handbook Must-Haves (California-Specific)
Generate a draft with Claude, have an employment attorney review ($500–$1,000 flat fee):
- At-will employment statement
- Harassment and discrimination policy (required in California)
- Meal and rest break policy
- Paid sick leave policy
- Time reporting and overtime approval policy
- Social media policy
- Tip policy (if applicable)
- Dispute resolution / arbitration agreement (discuss with attorney)

## Payroll Setup

### Recommended: Gusto
- $6/employee/month + $40 base
- Handles California daily OT automatically
- Tracks paid sick leave accrual
- Files all payroll taxes (CA EDD, CA SDI, FUTA, FICA)
- Direct deposit; paystubs are legally compliant
- Integrates with QuickBooks for expense tracking

### Payroll Frequency
- California requires at minimum semi-monthly (twice/month) payroll for most employees
- Weekly payroll is preferred by hourly workers and reduces end-of-period disputes

### Workers' Compensation
- Required from your first employee
- In California: rated on payroll × risk classification rate
- Restaurant industry rate: approximately $4–$8 per $100 of payroll
- Source quotes from: State Compensation Insurance Fund (SCIF), Employers Holdings, or your business insurance broker
- Report actual payroll annually; premiums adjust

## Common California HR Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Risk |
|---|---|
| Misclassifying employees as independent contractors | IRS + California EDD audit; back taxes + penalties |
| Not providing meal breaks or charging employees for them | PAGA lawsuit; $50–$100/violation/employee |
| Paying weekly OT instead of daily OT | Same PAGA exposure |
| Not keeping time records for 3 years | Shifts burden of proof to employer in disputes |
| Auto-deducting meal breaks without verifying they were taken | PAGA exposure |
| Docking pay for uniform requirements | California prohibits uniform deductions below minimum wage |
| Asking about criminal history before conditional offer | California "ban the box" law |
| Tip pooling with managers/supervisors | Illegal in California |
