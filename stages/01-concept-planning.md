# Stage 1: Concept & Market Research

**Timeline:** Months 1–2
**Goal:** Lock in a concept that has a clear customer, clear differentiation, and a viable unit economics model for San Jose before spending a dollar.

---

## Why Concept Comes Before Everything

Most restaurants fail not because of bad food — they fail because the concept didn't fit the market, the location, or the operator's actual skills. A 6-week research phase costs almost nothing and prevents a $400K mistake.

---

## San Jose Market Landscape

### Strengths of the Market
- **High average income:** Median household income in San Jose is ~$130K — customers can and will pay for quality
- **Tech worker density:** Tens of thousands of workers who order lunch 4–5x/week and value convenience over price
- **Diverse population:** 40% Asian, 33% Hispanic/Latino — creates demand for authentic cuisine and also a sophisticated palate
- **Delivery culture:** DoorDash and Uber Eats penetration is among the highest in the US
- **Corporate catering:** Hundreds of companies within delivery range of almost any location

### Weaknesses / Risks
- Very high labor costs (California laws)
- Competitive established restaurant scene — must differentiate clearly
- High commercial rents in desirable areas
- Long permitting timelines can delay opening by 3–6 months

### Underserved Gaps (As of 2025–2026)
Based on market analysis, these are less saturated:
- **High-quality breakfast/brunch** (beyond chains) in North SJ near tech campuses
- **Corporate-focused meal prep / healthy bowl concepts**
- **Authentic regional Mexican** (beyond Tex-Mex chains) — large population base
- **Late-night food** (Downtown SJ drops off after 10pm)
- **Premium ghost kitchen brand** in the fast-casual range ($15–$22 average check)

## Concept Development Framework

### The 5 Questions to Answer
1. **Who is the customer?** (Not "everyone" — pick one primary persona)
2. **What occasion does this solve?** (Weekday lunch, date night, family dinner, corporate catering)
3. **Why come here instead of the nearest competitor?** (Must be answerable in one sentence)
4. **What does an average ticket look like?** (Build backwards from labor/food costs)
5. **Can it scale?** (Catering arm? Second location? Ghost kitchen franchise?)

### Customer Persona Templates for San Jose

**Persona A: The Tech Lunch Crowd**
- Age 25–40, works at a startup or mid-size tech company 0.5–3 miles from your location
- Eats lunch out or orders delivery 4–5x/week
- Willing to spend $16–$22 for a quality, healthy, fast meal
- Values: speed (<8 min from order to food), quality ingredients, easy mobile ordering
- Discovery: Yelp, coworker recommendations, DoorDash

**Persona B: The Willow Glen Regular**
- Age 35–55, homeowner, family or couple
- Comes to restaurants for connection, not convenience
- Spends $25–$45/head including drinks
- Values: neighborhood feel, consistency, staff who remember them
- Discovery: word of mouth, local neighborhood groups, Instagram

**Persona C: The East SJ Community Customer**
- Age 25–50, working-class, price-conscious
- Wants authentic food, generous portions, value
- Average check $10–$16
- Values: authenticity, family-style portions, takeout-friendly
- Discovery: word of mouth, Spanish-language social media, Google Maps

## Concept Validation (Before Signing Anything)

### Step 1: Competitive Analysis
Walk or drive every competitor within 1.5 miles of your target location:
- What are they serving?
- What's the wait time at peak hours?
- What does Yelp say people love vs. hate?
- What's missing from every review?

Use Claude API to analyze the top 50 Yelp reviews of 5 competitors: "What do customers praise? What complaints appear 3+ times? What do they wish existed?"

### Step 2: Concept Test Without a Restaurant
Before opening, validate demand:
- **Farmers market stall:** $300–$500 to test on a weekend; real customers, real feedback, real revenue
- **Pop-up at a bar or brewery:** Many bars welcome guest kitchens Thu–Sat; no rent, split revenue
- **Ghost kitchen pilot:** Spin up a virtual brand on DoorDash for $0 using a commercial kitchen rental ($20–$40/hr)
- **Catering a few events:** Book 3–5 small catering jobs; validate food quality, logistics, and customer willingness to pay

**Success signal:** 3 months of pop-up/ghost kitchen data showing 30%+ repeat customers and a food cost you can actually hit.

### Step 3: Secret Shopper Analysis
Visit your 3 closest direct competitors as a customer:
- Time from entry to order, order to food
- Cleanliness, staff energy, table turn efficiency
- Receipt: understand their pricing and likely food cost %
- Take notes; this is your competitive baseline

### Step 4: Location-Concept Fit
Don't find a location and then pick a concept — pick the concept first, then find the location that fits. A ramen shop needs a hood vent and high gas capacity. A salad bar needs minimal cooking equipment. Match concept to what a space can support and what the neighborhood wants.

## Concept One-Pager Template

Before moving to business planning, write a one-page concept brief:

```
Concept Name: [Working name]
Cuisine / Format: [e.g., "Korean-American fast casual"]
Primary Customer: [One persona — be specific]
Price Point: [Average check with and without alcohol]
Occasion: [Lunch, dinner, late night, catering, all-day]
Unique Positioning: [One sentence — why us over the competition]
Proof of Demand: [What validation have you done]
Target Neighborhood: [And why]
Financial Model Summary: [Revenue target, food cost target, labor target]
Concept Risks: [Top 3 honest risks]
```

Lock this before moving to Stage 2.
