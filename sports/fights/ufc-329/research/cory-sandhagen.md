## Style Notes for This Matchup

### What Sandhagen Does Well vs. Bautista
- **Range control:** At 5'11" with 71" reach vs. Bautista's 5'9"/69", Sandhagen holds a 2" height and reach advantage — critical for establishing the jab and controlling distance
- **Counter-striking timing:** Sandhagen excels at timing entries with knees, elbows, and uppercuts. Bautista's pressure-forward style plays into Sandhagen's counter-attack game
- **Submission threat:** Sandhagen submitted Bautista with an armbar in Round 1 in their first meeting (UFC Brooklyn, Jan 19, 2019, 3:31 R1). He has a BJJ brown belt and dangerous guillotine
- **Footwork:** Sandhagen's lateral movement disrupts grapplers who need to close distance linearly. Bautista was stifled by Nurmagomedov's overwhelming wrestling, not by skilled footwork fighters

### Potential Vulnerabilities
- **Elite wrestling:** Nurmagomedov (11/14 TDs, 10+ min control) and Dvalishvili (20 TDs) exploited Sandhagen's vulnerability to sustained wrestling. Bautista averages 1.92 TDs/15 min — below elite wrestler output
- **Volume absorption:** 3.40 SApM suggests Sandhagen trades punches at times; Bautista's 5.33 SLpM means he can tag Sandhagen if range lapses
- **Cardio:** UFC 320 showed stamina issues under prolonged wrestling pressure; 3-round format at UFC 329 limits this concern significantly

## Situational Factors
- **Rest:** ~9 months since UFC 320 (Oct 2025 → Jul 2026) — full camp, healthy recovery
- **Motivation:** 2 losses in last 3 (both to elite wrestlers), seeking return to title picture; motivated to finish cleanly
- **Revenge factor:** None — Sandhagen already holds the win in fight 1
- **Schedule spot:** No look-ahead or letdown concerns; this is a meaningful ranked rematch
- **Experience edge:** 18–6 vs. 17–3; Sandhagen has fought at main event/PPV level twice (title shots vs. Yan and Dvalishvili)
- **3-round format:** Favors Sandhagen — his losses have come in 5-round wars against wrestlers; 3 rounds limits attrition
