## Fighting Style

**Unorthodox scrambler with elite submission grappling and capoeira-influenced striking**

Royval is one of the most entertaining and dangerous flyweights in UFC history. His style blends capoeira-influenced footwork and unpredictable striking angles with black-belt level Brazilian jiu-jitsu. He thrives in chaos — the more scrambled and frantic a fight becomes, the more comfortable he is. Royval is not a volume striker by design; he hunts for the decisive moment, either a debilitating punch from an odd angle or a lightning-fast submission transition. His BJJ coach Mario Correa and head coach Marc Montoya at Factory X have developed him into a complete grappler who can find submissions from almost any position.

### Grappling

Royval holds a BJJ black belt and it shows. He has 9 career submissions out of 17 wins — a 53% submission rate that is elite at any weight class. His preferred finishes are the armbar and rear-naked choke, though he is comfortable hunting triangles and guillotines from guard. He is an exceptional scrambler who converts takedown attempts against him into offensive opportunities; opponents who wrestle Royval can find themselves suddenly on the wrong end of a submission. His takedown defense (39%) looks poor on paper, but in practice he often invites the takedown knowing he can work offensively from his back. The trade-off: against elite wrestlers who control top position and limit scrambles, he is vulnerable to being ridden out for decisions.

### Striking

Royval's striking is not conventional but it is effective in bursts. He incorporates capoeira-style spinning attacks and unorthodox angles that make him difficult to time. He does not throw high volume but looks for specific, damaging shots — his knockout of Matheus Nicolau (Apr 2023, R1) showcased his ability to land a perfectly-timed punch from a deceptive angle. He is a southpaw, which creates natural angle advantages against orthodox opponents. His SApM of 2.63 is respectable, suggesting he is not walking into a lot of clean shots, but his striking defense (50%) and tendency to engage in brawls means he can be dropped if he gets careless. The Manel Kape KO (Dec 2025) is a prime example: a lunging right hand sat him down as he was pressuring forward.

### Cardio

Royval's cardio is above average but not the defining feature of his game the way it is for elite pressure fighters. He has had fights go to decision (UFC 296 vs. Pantoja, UFC 317 vs. Van), where he remained competitive in later rounds. However, his best performances tend to come in finishes — he is a different, more dangerous fighter when chasing a stoppage than when grinding out a decision. In the Van fight (UFC 317, 2025), both fighters landed 200+ significant strikes in a 3-round war, indicating he can sustain high output. He is not a fighter who relies on outlasting opponents; he relies on creating chaos and exploiting openings.

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. Kai Kara-France — UFC 253, Sep 27, 2020 (Win, Submission R2)
Royval's UFC coming-out party. Both fighters were knocked down in a wild first round. Royval survived, adapted, and submitted Kara-France in R2 with a triangle choke. Earned Fight of the Night and announced himself as a legitimate flyweight contender. This fight perfectly encapsulates his ability to thrive in chaos and find finishes from adversity.

### vs. Brandon Moreno — UFC 255, Nov 21, 2020 (Loss, UD3) and UFC FN 237, Feb 24, 2024 (Win, Split Dec)
The bookend rivalry that frames his career arc. He lost the first meeting via unanimous decision to the future champion. Three years later, after multiple top-ranked wins, he got the rematch and eked out a split decision — the first time Moreno had lost a non-title fight in the UFC. Shows Royval's long-term growth and willingness to fight the best.

### vs. Alexandre Pantoja — UFC 296, Dec 16, 2023 (Loss, UD5 — UFC Flyweight Title Fight)
Royval's career apex. He earned a title shot against the dominant champion and pushed Pantoja for 5 rounds, but came up short on all three scorecards. The performance revealed both his ceiling (competitive against the absolute best) and his floor (when forced into a wrestling-heavy decision fight against an equally technical grappler, his submission game is neutralized). The earlier meeting (UFC on ESPN 29, Aug 2021) also ended in a submission loss to Pantoja in R2.

### vs. Tatsuro Taira — UFC FN 244, Oct 12, 2024 (Win, Split Dec — Main Event)
One of the most important wins of Royval's career. Taira was undefeated and widely considered a future champion. Royval won a hard-fought split decision in the main event, earning his 5th Fight of the Night bonus and re-establishing himself as a top-3 flyweight. The win proved he could beat elite emerging talent and that his experience matters at this level.

### vs. Joshua Van — UFC 317, Jun 28, 2025 (Loss, UD3)
Made UFC history as the first fight where both fighters landed 200+ significant strikes. Royval was outworked in a three-round war by the younger Van, who took the fight on three weeks' notice. The fight earned Fight of the Night honors and was widely regarded as a 2025 Fight of the Year candidate, but the loss reinforced Royval's pattern: spectacular entertainment, but decisions go against him when he faces disciplined opponents who can out-volume or out-point him.

### Method of Victory Markets

- **Submission**: His most likely win method by far. 9 of 17 career wins (53%) come via sub. At +150 or better for "Royval by submission," this is frequently a playable prop.
- **KO/TKO**: He has 4 career KOs but it is not his primary path. The Nicolau KO (2023) showed he can finish on the feet, but do not bet on it at steep odds.
- **Decision**: He is 0–4 in UFC decisions. If a Royval fight goes to the cards, he typically loses. Fade Royval by decision props; back the opponent in decision markets.
- **Opponent by KO**: Royval has been finished by strikes (Pantoja technically, Kape definitively). Power punchers have a real path to a KO finish — this prop has value in the right matchup.

### Totals

- **Under 1.5 rounds**: Real value against him in the right matchup (power strikers, elite wrestlers who disengage). His Kape loss ended at 3:18 of R1.
- **Over 1.5 rounds**: Safe bet if his opponent is a submission specialist or defensively sound — he keeps it competitive.
- **Over 2.5 rounds**: Lean toward under in fights where his opponent has a defined physical advantage. His career has a bimodal distribution: quick finishes or grueling decisions.
- In fight wars (Van-style), the **over** on rounds and total significant strikes is live — he participates in action fights.
