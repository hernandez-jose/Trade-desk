**Timeline:** Months 7–9 (order 8–12 weeks before needed — lead times are long)
**Goal:** Equip the kitchen with the right tools at the right price, and lock in supplier relationships before opening day.

---

## Equipment Strategy

### New vs. Used
Never default to new. A used commercial refrigerator that's 3 years old is identical in function to a new one at 50–60% of the price. Buy used for everything that doesn't have moving parts or wear-sensitive components. Buy new for anything where failure causes immediate revenue loss (POS, primary range, walk-in refrigeration).

**Buy used:** Prep tables, sheet pan racks, shelving, smallwares, mixers, slicers, steam tables, dry storage
**Buy new (or refurbished from reputable dealer):** Walk-in cooler, primary cooking range, dishwasher, POS system

### Where to Buy Used Equipment in the Bay Area
- **CL Foodservice** (Santa Clara) — Large selection, some refurbished
- **Ace Mart Restaurant Supply** — Mix of new and used
- **Kairak / Commercial Refrigeration Repair SJ** — Used commercial refrigeration
- **AuctionZip + RestaurantEquipment.bid** — Liquidation auctions; best prices but as-is
- **Craigslist / Facebook Marketplace** — Gems exist; must inspect and test in person
- **WebstaurantStore / Amazon Business** — Good for smallwares, not major equipment

## Supplier Relationships

### Food Distributors (Bay Area)

**Broadline (everything):**
- **Sysco San Jose** — Largest; broadest selection; negotiate volume pricing
- **US Foods** — Strong alternative; competitive on pricing; good tech tools
- **Performance Food Group** — Good for specialty items

**Specialty / Local:**
- **Veritable Vegetable** (San Francisco) — Organic, sustainable produce; great for farm-to-table positioning
- **Four Star Seafood** — Premium Bay Area seafood
- **Specialty's** (now closed) / **Restaurant Depot** (East SJ on Ringwood) — Cash and carry; buy direct at wholesale for non-perishables
- **Bay Area Produce (Produce Terminal, San Francisco)** — If you're buying at volume

**Protein / Meat:**
- **Niman Ranch** — Premium, Bay Area brand; great for your story
- **Restaurant Depot** for commodity proteins
- **Pacific Gourmet** — Premium proteins, specialty items

### Negotiating with Distributors
- Get quotes from at least 2 broadline distributors before signing
- Ask for: weekly delivery schedule, minimum order (typically $300–$500 Sysco), credit terms (net 30 if possible — don't pay COD if you can help it)
- Lock in pricing for 90 days on high-volume items; commodity items (produce, proteins) fluctuate
- Start small, prove payment reliability, then negotiate better pricing at 90 days

### AI-Assisted Ordering (BlueCart Integration)
Once you have MarketMan tracking your inventory, BlueCart can automatically generate and send purchase orders to your distributors when stock hits par level. This eliminates manual ordering, reduces stockouts, and prevents over-ordering that drives food waste.

## Equipment Leasing vs. Buying

For the most expensive items (walk-in, hood, dish machine), leasing is an option:
- Preserves capital for working capital
- Maintenance often included in lease
- Typical terms: 36–60 months; effective rate 6–15%
- Downside: You don't own it; harder to sell the business

**Recommendation:** Buy used for most items; lease only if cash is critically constrained. Avoid equipment leasing from restaurant supply companies with high rates.
