## Style Notes for This Matchup

### Striking
- Pimblett will have a striking accuracy advantage on paper (52% vs. Saint-Denis's 41% defense)
- His head movement is poor — he stands straight up and relies on his chin more than his guard
- SApM of 4.96 is concerning against a pressure southpaw like BSD who lands 5.17/min
- Against Gaethje he abandoned his grappling to trade strikes — he has publicly vowed not to repeat that ego-driven mistake
- His jab-cross combinations and forward pressure can accumulate damage over 15 minutes if he keeps the fight standing
- Key risk: Saint-Denis's southpaw left hand (same side as an orthodox fighter's power hand) is his best KO weapon — Pimblett's straight-up stance is vulnerable to left hooks

### Grappling
- Pimblett's BJJ is legitimate (first-degree black belt) and his submission average (1.7/15 min) is significantly higher than Saint-Denis's (0.70)
- His back attack game is elite — if he can scramble behind BSD, the RNC is his primary threat
- His takedown defense in UFC has been perfect (100%), which will be tested against BSD's elite wrestling (3.97 TD avg)
- If Pimblett can stop the takedown, he can invert the grappling dynamic and hunt for his own submissions
- Key risk: BSD's 3.97 TD average is the highest Pimblett will have ever faced; if BSD gets him down early, Pimblett faces arm-triangles and chokes that have finished 11 opponents

## Situational Factors
- **Motivation:** First fight since losing the interim LW title shot. Pimblett has publicly committed to fighting smarter, using his grappling, and not letting ego drive decisions again.
- **Ranking position:** #6 (vs. #5 BSD) — a win puts Pimblett back in title picture immediately
- **Schedule/Rest:** ~5.5 months since UFC 324 (Jan 24 → Jul 11). Adequate recovery from a brutal 5-round war.
- **Location:** Las Vegas (T-Mobile Arena) — Pimblett fought well in LV previously (Gaethje fight)
- **Round length:** 3 rounds — favors Pimblett's gassing concerns; he showed cardio issues in 5-round fights
- **Fan support:** Pimblett has massive following; co-main event billing at International Fight Week will generate significant crowd support — potential psychological edge
- **Poirier's take:** Dustin Poirier (who beat BSD at UFC 299) is picking Pimblett to win, citing BSD's vulnerability to counterpunching and pressure striking
