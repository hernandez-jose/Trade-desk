## Fighting Style

**Wrestling-based pressure fighter with high-volume striking**

Covington is built around three pillars:
1. **Relentless forward pressure** — he never lets opponents settle or breathe
2. **Volume striking to set up takedowns** — not a power puncher, but throws 8–10 punch combos to close distance
3. **Elite cardio** — arguably the best gas tank in welterweight history; gets *stronger* in rounds 3–5

### Grappling
- NCAA Division I wrestler (Oregon State)
- High-volume takedown attempts — will shoot 10–15 times per round
- Accuracy is moderate (~39%) but volume overwhelms opponents
- Once on top: good ground-and-pound, stays busy, grinds opponents
- Submission game is minimal — he wins from top position with ground strikes

### Striking
- High output, not high power
- Left jab and straight right are primary weapons
- Uses strikes primarily to close distance for takedowns
- No knockout power in his hands — his TKO wins come from ground-and-pound after a takedown
- **Vulnerable**: Can be caught with power shots when he's pressuring — his head movement is limited

### Cardio
His defining weapon. In 5-round fights, Covington's pace is elite:
- Round 1: High pace to measure the opponent
- Round 2–3: Most fights peak here — he absorbs punishment
- Round 4–5: He gets *better* while opponents fade — this is where he closes fights

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. Rafael dos Anjos — UFC 225 (Win, UD5)
Covington's breakout performance. Won the interim WW title. Dominated dos Anjos for 5 rounds with relentless pressure and volume. Showcased his elite cardio blueprint for the first time at the highest level.

### vs. Kamaru Usman — UFC 245 (Loss, TKO R5)
Covington's most competitive performance — he was arguably winning the fight going into Round 5, but was stopped with 51 seconds left. This fight established both his ceiling (elite pressure) and his floor (chin is vulnerable to sustained power shots late in fights).

### vs. Tyron Woodley — UFC FN 178 (Win, TKO R5)
Covington got dropped in Round 3, recovered, and finished the fight in Round 5 with ground-and-pound. A signature display of mental resilience and cardio.

### vs. Kamaru Usman — UFC 268 (Loss, UD5)
Five-round war. Covington pushed Usman harder than any opponent had. Lost the decision but showed he belongs in championship bouts. Debatable scoring.

### vs. Leon Edwards — UFC 296 (Loss, TKO R3)
Stopped in the third round — his most damaging loss in terms of career trajectory. Edwards exposed Covington's head movement with cleaner boxing. At 35, the chin is clearly a concern.
