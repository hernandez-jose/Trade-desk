North San Jose's residential population of ~57K **understates the market dramatically**. The daytime population swells significantly because of the tech campus density.

**The opportunity:** Large campus employers (Cisco, Google, Adobe) have subsidized cafeterias that absorb a large portion of their workers' meals. But:
1. Workers still leave campus for variety, client meals, and team outings
2. Startups and mid-size companies in the same corridor (15,000–30,000 workers estimated) have **no subsidized food**
3. Corporate catering for meetings and off-sites bypasses the cafeteria entirely

### The Real Target: Non-Campus Tech Workers

The most underserved customer in North San Jose is the employee of a 50–500-person tech company in an office park off Zanker, North First, or Trimble — no cafeteria, often no nearby walkable food, ordering lunch 4x/week via DoorDash. This customer:
- Has a $20–$25 lunch budget
- Wants healthy, satisfying, customizable food
- Orders via app — physical discovery is secondary
- Will champion a great spot to the entire office if they love it
- Is the entry point to corporate catering contracts

## Food Delivery Market in North San Jose

### Platform Landscape
- **DoorDash** and **Uber Eats** are the dominant platforms; EZCater dominates corporate catering orders
- San Jose has among the highest delivery platform penetration in the US (tech-native consumer base)
- Ghost kitchen viability is high in this corridor due to strong delivery density even at lower residential density

### Most-Ordered Delivery Cuisines in Silicon Valley (2025–2026)
1. Chinese / Asian fusion
2. Mexican / tacos
3. Pizza
4. Sushi / Japanese
5. Indian
6. Burgers
7. Poke / Hawaiian
8. Mediterranean / Middle Eastern

**Observation:** The cuisines Silicon Valley workers order most heavily skew **Asian and international**. The large Asian professional population both drives demand for authentic Asian food and creates demand for fusion formats that bridge Asian flavors with Western convenience formats (burritos, bowls, wraps).

### Corporate Catering Demand (2025 Data)
- **53%** of companies now cater lunch at least once per week — up from 38% pre-pandemic
- **60%** of corporate food orderers plan to spend **more** on workplace food in 2025
- Nearly **1 in 3** expect budget increases of **25%+**
- Bay Area lunch catering average: **$20–$21 per person**
- Workers aged 18–29: **71% receive regular workplace catering** — this cohort expects it
- San Jose tech catering accounts: typically 20–200 people per order, weekly or bi-weekly frequency

## San Jose Restaurant Market Context

### Record Growth (But Uneven)
- 933 business tax certificates issued for new restaurants, cafes, and bakeries citywide between 2022 and October 2025
- Q1 2025: Highest restaurant sales tax revenue in city history — **21% above pre-pandemic**
- New openings concentrated in: Downtown (231 new spots), Little Saigon, Willow Glen, Japantown, The Alameda
- **North San Jose notably absent from this growth wave** — creating the opportunity

### What's Growing in the Broader SJ Market
- Vietnamese street food (Bui Vien Street Food in Little Saigon)
- Japanese restaurant-marketplace hybrids (Yutori concept)
- Mediterranean new formats (Eos & Nyx)
- Authentic regional Mexican (not Tex-Mex)
- All-you-can-eat premium experiences (Wagyu Master)
- Specialty coffee + food hybrids

### What's Closing and Why (2025 Lessons)
According to McFadden-Finch Group analysis of 2025 closures:
- **Rising operational costs** outpacing revenue — primarily labor and energy
- **Lease terminations / rent increases** forcing closure despite healthy revenue
- **Slow downtown office return** hurt destination restaurants in office districts
- **Insufficient working capital** — opened undercapitalized
- **Concept-market mismatch** — wrong food for wrong neighborhood
- **Regulatory complications** — especially in Japantown (historic designations)

**North San Jose implication:** The tech corridor's hybrid work model means weekday lunch traffic is present but not five days/week. A concept that runs lunch delivery + in-person + catering is more resilient than a pure dine-in concept. Build for multiple revenue streams.

## Cultural Context: Why North San Jose Is Different

### The Asian-Majority Professional Market
51.9% Asian population with 38% non-citizen residents means North San Jose has:
- **Chinese (mainland, Taiwanese, Cantonese)** — largest single group; very quality-conscious
- **Indian (South Indian, Punjabi, Bengali)** — large tech worker community; vegetarian needs; major catering customer
- **Vietnamese** — significant community; deep pho/Vietnamese food loyalty; underserved in N. SJ specifically
- **Korean** — tech worker population; drives Korean BBQ and Korean fried chicken demand
- **Persian / Iranian** — significant in the Cisco/Broadcom engineer population; almost no Persian food in N. SJ
- **Filipino** — growing; comfort food demand
- **Japanese** — small but high-spending; quality-sensitive

**The opportunity this creates:** A concept that authentically serves one of these communities, or intelligently bridges multiple (e.g., a pan-Asian bowl concept), has a built-in loyal customer base from day one.

### What North San Jose Workers Have Said (Aggregated Review Themes)
Based on reviews of existing North SJ restaurants, workers consistently express:
- "Finally something better than Subway or the cafeteria"
- "I wish there were more options around here"
- "Great for when I need a real meal, not just fast food"
- "Drive 15 minutes to Santana Row when I want something nicer — wish there was something closer"
- Complaints about Curry Up Now: "small portions for the price," "confusing menu"
- Complaints about the area generally: "limited options for breakfast," "nothing good late night"

## Conclusion: The North San Jose Opportunity

North San Jose is a **market with world-class purchasing power and world-class underservice in the restaurant category.** The daytime population of tens of thousands of professional workers — earning $150K+ household income, eating out 4–6x/week, heavy on delivery and corporate catering — is being served by a handful of mostly casual, mostly Korean or Indian options with limited delivery infrastructure and minimal corporate catering capability.

The white space is: **a fast-casual or delivery-first concept that is health-forward, has clear and excellent food (simple menu), actively courts corporate catering from day one, and builds a digital-first customer relationship through a strong AI-powered marketing and operations stack.**

The lower rent in North SJ (vs. Willow Glen, Santana Row) means you can get to profitability faster. The tech-native customer means digital ordering, delivery, and loyalty programs work harder here than almost anywhere in California.
