# UFC / MMA Betting — Overview & Strategy

## Why MMA is One of the Best Markets for Sharp Bettors

- **Low liquidity** vs. team sports → books make more pricing errors
- **Public heavily bets names** (McGregor, Jones, O'Malley) → fade opportunities
- **Style matchups** are more decisive than in any other sport — a pure wrestler vs. a pure striker is heavily predictable
- **Late-breaking information** (weight cuts, injury news, camp rumors) moves lines dramatically

## Core UFC Bet Types

### 1. Moneyline (Fight Winner)
The standard bet. Who wins the fight by any method.

```
Convert to implied probability:
  -200 → 200/300 = 66.7% (must win 66.7% to break even)
  +170 → 100/270 = 37.0%
```

### 2. Method of Victory
- **KO/TKO**: Fighter wins by knockout or technical knockout (ref stoppage)
- **Submission**: Fighter wins by choke, armbar, heel hook, etc.
- **Decision**: Fight goes to the judges (split, majority, or unanimous)

These markets offer significant edges because books price them poorly. A high-volume wrestler who wins 80% by decision is often mispriced in decision prop markets.

### 3. Round Betting
- **Fight to go the distance (Yes/No)**: Does the fight reach the final round?
- **Over/Under rounds**: Does it pass the posted round number (e.g., 2.5)?
- **Exact round finish**: Higher odds, harder to hit, but clear edges in style mismatches

### 4. Total Rounds (O/U)
- Main card 5-round fights: typical line is 2.5 or 3.5
- Prelim 3-round fights: typical line is 1.5 or 2.5
- When a pure striker faces a pure grappler who takes them down early: lean under

### 5. Parlay Strategy
- Two-fight parlays are common in MMA — books price individual fights, but correlated parlays are allowed
- Limit to 2-leg parlays in MMA; variance is extreme with more legs
- Never parlay two heavy favorites (combined implied prob >85%) — too much juice for too little return

## Fight Analysis Framework

### Step 1: Identify the Style Matchup
MMA has clear style hierarchies. Assess each fighter on:

| Skill Area | Rating 1–10 | Key Questions |
|------------|-------------|---------------|
| Boxing | 1–10 | Head movement, combinations, power |
| Kickboxing/Muay Thai | 1–10 | Leg kicks, clinch, elbows |
| Wrestling | 1–10 | Takedown offense AND defense |
| Jiu-Jitsu/Submissions | 1–10 | Guard, submissions, grappling IQ |
| Chin/Durability | 1–10 | Has he been hurt or stopped before? |
| Cardio/Endurance | 1–10 | Does performance decline in late rounds? |

### Step 2: Determine Who Controls the Engagement
MMA comes down to one question: **who gets to fight the fight they want?**

- Wrestler vs. Striker → Wrestler controls if takedown defense is poor
- Southpaw vs. Orthodox → Check H2H record against each stance
- Pressure fighter vs. Counter-striker → Does the counter-striker have good footwork?
- Submission specialist vs. Grappler → Who controls position?

### Step 3: Check the Stats
Refer to `sports/ufc/fighters.md` for:
- SLpM (strikes landed per minute) — offensive output
- Striking accuracy — quality of strikes
- SApM (strikes absorbed per minute) — how much damage they take
- TD avg — takedown output
- TD defense — ability to stay standing
- Sub avg — submission threat

### Step 4: Situational Factors
| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Short-notice replacement (<2 weeks) | Fade the replacement fighter |
| Weight cut failure (missed weight) | Fade the fighter who missed |
| Long layoff (12+ months) | Rust factor; lean against them in early rounds |
| Moving up in weight class | Fade unless they have a massive size advantage |
| Moving down in weight class | Back — extra power and speed |
| UFC debut (first fight in the org) | Variance is high; avoid unless huge line value |
| Consecutive losses (2+) | Back as underdog — desperate fighters are dangerous |

### Step 5: Odds vs. True Probability
Same EV framework as all sports:
- Calculate your true win probability using the style analysis
- Convert to American odds
- If Klashi/Kalshi's implied probability is 10%+ off your estimate: bet it

## Key MMA Betting Angles

### 1. Style Mismatch (Most Important)
The clearest edge in UFC betting:

| Matchup | Historical Lean |
|---------|----------------|
| Elite wrestler vs. striker without takedown defense | Back the wrestler |
| Elite jiu-jitsu vs. one-dimensional wrestler | Back the jiu-jitsu fighter |
| Southpaw vs. orthodox who struggles with southpaws | Check career stats vs. southpaws |
| Pressure fighter vs. shot chin | Back the pressure fighter — they'll get the finish |
| Technical striker vs. wild brawler | Back the technician for decisions |

### 2. Fade the Big Name
The public overbets:
- Jon Jones (always a massive favorite)
- Conor McGregor (when active — massive public betting skews line)
- Any fighter returning from a highlight-reel win
- Champions defending for the first time (overconfidence factor)

Books know this and shade the line, but the public overbets anyway. When a name fighter is at -400 or worse, evaluate whether the true probability justifies the price.

### 3. Southpaw Advantage
~10–12% of fighters are left-handed (southpaw). Orthodox fighters face southpaws rarely — they have less experience and less film. Southpaw fighters often have positive statistical edges against orthodox opponents in:
- Striking accuracy
- Takedown defense (awkward angles)
- Landing the left cross / right kick

### 4. Late Replacement
When a fighter takes a fight on short notice (<2 weeks):
- Camp is incomplete; game plan is rushed
- Cardio is often not at peak
- Weight cut may be extreme and fast
- **Fade late replacements** unless they are a massive natural size/skill upgrade

### 5. Weight Cut Impact
MMA fighters cut significant water weight to make their division. Signs of a hard weight cut:
- Fighter misses weight (automatic red flag)
- Fighter looks depleted at weigh-ins
- History of missing weight or looking drained

Fighters who miss weight or look depleted are statistically more likely to fade in the 2nd and 3rd rounds.

### 6. The Debut Fighter Trap
New UFC fighters are often overpriced:
- Their competition level on the regional circuit was significantly lower
- The UFC's pace, power, and precision is a step above
- Books often price debuts too short on both sides due to limited data
- Value is usually on the experienced UFC fighter vs. a debuting opponent

### 7. Revenge Fight Dynamics
Unlike team sports, MMA revenge fights are actually **less predictive**:
- The loser from the first fight watched the film obsessively and made adjustments
- Books price revenge fights at similar odds to the first fight
- In rematches, the underdog from fight 1 covers at a higher rate than in initial fights
- **Back the rematch underdog** — especially if fight 1 was close or a controversial decision

## Live In-Fight Betting

UFC live betting is available on Klashi/Kalshi during the fight. Key angles:

### Between-Round Markets
Lines reset between rounds. These are the best value in UFC betting:
- A fighter who landed a knockdown in Round 1 is sometimes priced too heavily
- A ground specialist who failed to take the fight down in Round 1 often re-prices too far toward the striker
- Always bet between-round lines, not mid-round (books widen the spread during action)

### Fighter Fatigue Tell
If a fighter is breathing heavily at the end of Round 1 in a 3-round fight:
- Lean toward their opponent in Rounds 2–3
- Back the over if both fighters are fatigued

### First-Round Survival
If the "dangerous early" fighter (known for early finishes) hasn't finished by the end of Round 2:
- Their "danger window" is partially closed
- Fade the live moneyline; back their opponent for later rounds

## Championship vs. Non-Championship Fight Differences

| Factor | 3-Round Fight | 5-Round Fight |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| Cardio weight | Moderate | Critical |
| Grappler advantage | Moderate | Strong — tire opponents out |
- **5-round fights**: Grapplers and high-cardio fighters are significantly undervalued in moneyline markets
- **3-round fights**: Explosive strikers are more dangerous — they don't need to pace themselves
