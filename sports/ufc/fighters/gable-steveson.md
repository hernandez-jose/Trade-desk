## Wrestling / Athletic Background

Gable Steveson is arguably the most accomplished amateur wrestler ever to enter the UFC. His mother named him after legendary wrestling coach Dan Gable.

**High School (Apple Valley, MN):**
- 4× Minnesota state champion
- Finished second in the state tournament as an **8th grader**
- Career high school record: 210–3 (98.59% win rate), highest in Minnesota history

**University of Minnesota (2019–2022 / 2024–2025):**
- **2× NCAA Division I National Champion** (285 lbs, 2021 and 2022)
- **4× Big Ten Conference Champion** — first heavyweight in conference history to win four titles
- **2× Dan Hodge Trophy winner** (award for the most dominant collegiate wrestler regardless of weight class — the Heisman of wrestling)
- **5× All-American**
- Career collegiate record: **85–2** (.977 win pct.), including a perfect 48–0 in dual competition
- 36–0 vs. Big Ten opponents

**Olympics:**
- **2020 Tokyo Olympic Games: Gold Medal** at 125 kg (super heavyweight)
- Became the **youngest freestyle wrestler to win Olympic gold at super heavyweight** at 21 years, 2 months, 6 days
- Won with a walk-off takedown in the final second of the match vs. Geno Petriashvili (Georgia) — one of the most dramatic finishes in Olympic wrestling history

**Post-College / Pre-MMA:**
- Signed with **WWE** in 2022; made TV debut at The Great American Bash (July 2023) vs. Baron Corbin — received a mixed reception; never found a major on-screen role
- Released by WWE on May 4, 2024
- Briefly explored an **NFL career** following WWE release
- Began MMA training with Jon Jones and Jackson-Wink in 2024–2025
- Made MMA debut September 2025; compiled 4–0 record on the regional circuit before UFC signing was announced April 11, 2026 at UFC 327

## Fighting Style

Steveson is a grappling-dominant, physically elite heavyweight whose wrestling base is unlike anything the UFC has seen in terms of pure credentials. His four regional wins all came via knockout or TKO — not from takedowns — which signals he is actively developing a striking game rather than relying solely on his wrestling. At this early stage, his offense features powerful looping punches and ground-and-pound after achieving the clinch or takedown. His style is explosive and physical. He carries significant punching power, and his athleticism — rare speed and agility for a 260+ lb man — creates problems for traditionally plodding heavyweights. His development under Jon Jones appears to be accelerating fast, but he remains a raw MMA fighter with under 10 minutes of total professional fight time.

### Grappling
Elite-tier credentialed wrestler, though MMA grappling and Olympic freestyle wrestling have meaningful differences. Steveson's folkstyle, freestyle, and overall body mechanics give him the tools to be a dominant grappler at heavyweight. He has a deep understanding of level changes, underhook battles, and takedown chains. His body control and explosiveness on scrambles should be well above the UFC heavyweight average. He has not yet been tested in the cage against anyone with serious submission offense. How his wrestling translates to MMA — particularly takedown defense against experienced opponents and guard passing against active submission threats — is the unknown. He has no documented submission finishes and no documented submission defense situations through four fights.

### Striking
Striking is the development area. Steveson has shown genuine knockout power: a 15-second KO of Billy Swanson and a 24-second KO of Kevin Hein are impressive in terms of power output, though both opponents were very limited. His right hand appears to be his primary weapon. His striking defense, however, exposed a concern — in the MFL 3 fight vs. Lezama (Feb 2026), he absorbed a clean spinning wheel kick to the head and showed minimal evasion before the shot landed. He ate the shot, showed chin durability by walking through it, and finished the fight — but the defensive lapse was visible and was reported widely by MMA outlets. Against UFC-caliber heavyweights with elite striking, his head movement and guard will need to be significantly tighter. He is working with Jon Jones, who is one of the most technically complete MMA fighters of all time, which is a credible development path, but the timeline is abbreviated.

### Cardio
Unknown and largely untested. Steveson has never gone past 3:50 of Round 1 in any professional fight. His wrestling background — particularly at the super heavyweight level where he competed explosively for 6-minute periods — suggests a reasonable aerobic base, but heavyweight MMA cardio is its own discipline. There is no data on how he performs in Rounds 2–3, which is a genuine unknown when pricing him for longer fight markets.

## Betting Profile

>

His Olympic pedigree and Jon Jones mentorship are legitimate, but bettors must price the fight on what is actually known, not narrative. His opponent, Elisha Ellison (5–2), while a heavy underdog, has UFC experience and all his finishes come early. The sportsbooks and casual public will price Steveson based on his wrestling legacy, not his 10 minutes of MMA fighting. This creates a structural overpricing risk on Steveson's moneyline.
