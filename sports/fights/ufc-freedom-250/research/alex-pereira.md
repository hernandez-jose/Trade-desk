# Alex Pereira — Deep Fight Research
## UFC Freedom 250 | vs. Ciryl Gane | June 14, 2026 | Heavyweight (Interim Title) | 5 Rounds

> Analyst note: Pereira is moving up to heavyweight for the first time at 38, but he is NOT giving up size (weighed 251 vs Gane's 248). His entire case is one-shot KO power against a low-power mover. His entire risk is being outpointed over 25 minutes by the best footwork in the division — or, less likely, controlled by Gane's underused wrestling. His most likely path to victory is a finish; his most likely path to defeat is a decision.
> Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary
**The case FOR Pereira:** He is the most lethal one-punch finisher in the upper weight classes, with a GLORY kickboxing pedigree, 62% striking accuracy, and a calf kick that has crippled mobility-based opponents (Rountree, Adesanya II). Gane carries low knockout power and has historically frozen against punchers (the Ngannou loss). Pereira neutralized the expected size disadvantage by weighing in at 251 lbs, three pounds heavier than Gane. He only needs to land one clean shot across 25 minutes.

**The case AGAINST Pereira:** This is his heavyweight debut at age 38. He is a low-volume striker (SLpM 5.0) with mediocre 50% striking defense and improving-but-questionable cardio over five rounds — and Gane is the best lateral mover and one of the best five-round gas tanks at heavyweight. If Gane sticks and moves and refuses to engage, Pereira can get out-touched on the scorecards. Pereira has also never faced a heavyweight's clinch/wrestling pressure; Gane has light takedowns he could mix in.

**Most likely path:** KO/TKO in rounds 1–3 if he lands clean early; if not, he risks dropping a decision. (Sources: [UFCStats](http://ufcstats.com/), [Bloody Elbow](https://bloodyelbow.com/2026/05/09/alex-pereira-reveals-huge-weight-he-plans-to-be-at-for-his-heavyweight-debut-at-ufc-white-house/), [SI](https://www.si.com/fannation/mma/news/alex-pereira-fight-week-weight-revealed-ufc-white-house-heavyweight-debut))

He has fought and won at championship distance (5-round TKO of Adesanya at UFC 281). The open question is purely *heavyweight* competition — this is his first 265 opponent.

## 3. Opponent Context
Gane is the toughest *stylistic* puzzle Pereira has faced: a 6'5", 81"-reach mover who does not stand in the pocket and rarely gets hit (2.25 SApM, 63% defense). Everything Pereira does well (counter power, pocket exchanges, calf kicks on a planted opponent) is harder to execute against an opponent who is constantly circling and refusing to engage. Conversely, Gane's low power and history of tentativeness against punchers (Ngannou) means he is unlikely to hurt Pereira and may fight not-to-lose. The wildcard is Gane's wrestling: he barely uses it (0.62 TD/15) but it is the one area where he could steal control rounds against Pereira's untested-at-HW takedown defense.

---

## 4. Striking Analysis
### Offense
Low-volume, ultra-high-quality. Long jab, calf kick, and a left hook / left-hand counter with fight-ending power. He hunts the single perfect shot rather than out-working opponents. 62% accuracy is elite.

### Defense
The weak spot. 50% striking defense and 3.65 SApM — he stands in the pocket and absorbs to land. Against a low-power mover this matters less (he can afford to eat soft jabs), but it means a high-volume opponent banks points.

### Historical Striking Data
SLpM 5.00, accuracy 62%, SApM 3.65, defense 50% ([UFCStats](http://ufcstats.com/)). The accuracy/power profile is championship-grade; the volume and defense numbers are why a mover can frustrate him.

---

## 5. Grappling Analysis
### Offensive Wrestling
Essentially none (0.19 TD/15). He does not shoot. Zero career submission wins.

### Defensive Wrestling
TD defense 70% on paper, but never tested against an elite heavyweight clinch/wrestler. He was grounded and controlled at points by Błachowicz and in the Ankalaev I loss. At HW the bodies are bigger and the control harder to escape.

### Scrambles & Submissions
Limited. He is not a submission threat and is not known for slick scrambles; if put on his back by a bigger man, he survives rather than reverses.

### Cage Wrestling
Untested at heavyweight. This is a genuine unknown, though Gane is not the wrestler to fully exploit it.

---

## 6. Cardio & Durability
### Pace Sustainability
Improved but still a question over five rounds — he faded somewhat late vs Adesanya I and looked flat in the Ankalaev I decision loss. Now carrying ~251 lbs of mass, sustaining output for 25 minutes against a tireless mover is a real concern.

### Recovery After Damage
Good chin historically (7/10 durability); his only KO loss was to Adesanya (UFC 287). Gane lacks the power to test it.

### Weight Cut Effects
None — this is a move UP, no cut. He weighed in healthy at 251. No depletion risk; arguably fresher than at a hard LHW cut.

### Injury History
No major active injury reported entering this bout. Full, healthy camp at Teixeira MMA.

## 10. Historical Context
### Style Matchup History
Pereira beats opponents who engage and plant (Adesanya, Hill, Procházka, Strickland, Rountree). His one decision loss (Ankalaev I) came against a wrestler/pressure fighter who took away his timing — not a mover, but a cautionary template for a fight where he can't land clean.

### Comparable Opponent Analysis
Gane is the Ngannou template *in reverse*: where Ngannou's power made Gane tentative, here PEREIRA is the puncher. The relevant comp for Gane's risk is "low-power but elite-moving striker vs heavy hitter," which historically tilts toward whoever imposes their fight.

### Career Arc
At the apex — two-division champ chasing an unprecedented third belt, on a violent finishing streak, at age 38.

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: One-shot KO, rounds 1–3 (HIGH likelihood, ~45–50% of his win equity)
Pereira corners Gane, lands the left hook or counter right as Gane resets, and finishes. His prop (+140) reflects this is the most likely single outcome of the fight.

### Path 2: Calf-kick attrition into a late finish / damage decision (MODERATE)
He chops Gane's lead leg, kills the lateral movement, and either finishes late or banks enough damage rounds to win a decision.

### Path 3: Win a tactical decision (LOW)
Out of character — he'd need to out-point the better volume striker. Unlikely unless he hurts Gane repeatedly without finishing.

## 12. X-Factors
- **Heavyweight debut at 38** — unknown how the bigger bodies, 4-oz HW power exchanges, and 5 rounds at 251 lbs affect him.
- **Cage size / outdoor White House setup** — a larger or unusual cage helps a mover (Gane); a smaller one helps the cage-cutter (Pereira). Confirm cage dimensions if available.
- **Gane's mindset** — if Gane fights tentatively (as vs Ngannou), Pereira's danger compounds.
- **Calf kick** — if it lands early and Gane's movement degrades, the whole fight flips Pereira's way.

## 13. Risk Factors & Confidence Assessment
### Primary Risks to Pereira Winning
1. Gane sticks and moves for 25 minutes and out-volumes him (his most likely loss path).
2. Cardio/output sag in rounds 4–5 carrying HW mass against a tireless mover.
3. Gane mixes in takedowns to steal control rounds against untested HW TD defense.

### Confidence Level
This is a true coin flip that the model nudges slightly toward Gane on the *who-controls-the-engagement* question (a mover dictating range over 5 rounds), offset by Pereira's superior finishing threat. **Model estimate for Pereira: ~46–48%.** His value, if any, is on the **KO/TKO method prop**, not the moneyline.
