# Christian Rodriguez — Deep Fight Research
## UFC Fight Night: Kape vs. Horiguchi (UFC Vegas 119) | vs. Hyder Amil | June 20, 2026 | 145 lbs | 3 Rounds (main card)

> Analyst note: Classic wrestler-vs-volume-striker style clash. Rodriguez's edge is his ability to change levels against an opponent (Amil) whose lone clear, repeatedly-exposed weakness is takedown defense and ground control. The market (Rodriguez -150/-155) already prices that read; the question is whether there's any leftover edge.
> Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR Rodriguez:** He is the more proven UFC commodity (5–4 UFC) with a genuine wrestling base, 100%-cited takedown accuracy, and a live submission game (4 career subs, ~3.0 sub avg). Amil's most-exploited flaw is exactly Rodriguez's best tool — Jamall Emmers just took Amil down 7 times for 8:35 of control over three rounds. Rodriguez can replicate that template.
**AGAINST Rodriguez:** He is low-volume on the feet (SLpM ~1.73, ~43% accuracy), is on a two-fight skid (close decision losses to Costa and Fili), and his own takedown defense (~43%) is shaky — but Amil rarely shoots, so that flaw is largely neutralized here. If the fight stays standing, Amil's pace and power win exchanges and rounds.
**Most likely path:** Rodriguez secures takedowns and control time, wins a grappling-led decision, with a live submission upside if Amil gives up his back scrambling.

*(Sources: Sherdog, Tapology, UFC.com, Cageside Press, LowKickMMA, KTVU)*

## 3. Opponent Context
Amil (11–2) is a high-volume, switch-stance pressure striker with elite cardio and KO power (6 of 11 wins by KO/TKO), but a glaring, twice-exposed grappling hole: Emmers controlled him for 8:35 on 7 takedowns, and Delgado iced him in 26 seconds standing. Amil does not shoot, so Rodriguez's mediocre TD defense is largely irrelevant. Amil's threat to Rodriguez is purely on the feet — if Rodriguez can't get it down and has to trade at range, Amil's output and power are the more dangerous tools.

## 4. Striking Analysis
### Offense
Low-volume (SLpM ~1.73, ~43% accuracy). Uses strikes to set up level changes rather than to win firefights. Clearly the lesser striker in this matchup on volume and power.
### Defense
Mediocre striking defense (~49%) — hittable by busy strikers, which is a concern against Amil's pace if the fight stalls on the feet.
### Historical Striking Data
SLpM ~1.73, accuracy ~43%, striking defense ~49% (aggregator figures; verify on UFCStats). Per-fight: 95/190 sig strikes vs Fili, 77/113 vs Costa, 55/93 vs Bashi.

## 5. Grappling Analysis

Strong. High-school wrestling base; cited 100% takedown accuracy (22/22). This is his win condition and Amil's worst phase.
### Defensive Wrestling
Below-average (~43% TD defense) — a real flaw, but Amil almost never shoots, so it should not be a factor here.
### Scrambles & Submissions
Excellent. ~3.0 sub avg, 4 career submission wins (RNC, armbar, anaconda + 1). Live finishing threat once the fight hits the mat; Amil has near-zero defensive grappling pedigree.
### Cage Wrestling
Uses combinations to close distance and chain to takedowns against the fence. Effective template available given Amil's TD defense.

## 10. Historical Context
### Style Matchup History
Rodriguez is at his best vs. strikers he can drag into grappling, and at his worst vs. stronger wrestlers (Costa out-grappled him). Amil is the former archetype, which favors Rodriguez.
### Comparable Opponent Analysis
Jamall Emmers is the cleanest comp: a featherweight who beat Amil by mixing takedowns (7) and control (8:35) into a striking game. Rodriguez is a better wrestler than Emmers, suggesting an even more grappling-tilted version of that win is available.
### Career Arc

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: Grappling-led decision (most likely)
Repeated level changes, takedowns, top control and positional dominance → clear-cut decision. The Emmers blueprint.
### Path 2: Submission (live secondary)
Amil's near-total lack of defensive grappling means a back-take/RNC or scramble submission is realistically on the table once it's on the mat.
### Path 3: Out-point standing (least likely)
Unlikely given volume gap — if forced to strike, Rodriguez is the underdog in that phase.

---

## 12. X-Factors
- **Can he get it down?** Amil's TD defense is bad but he has cardio and movement; if Rodriguez can't secure early takedowns and the fight lingers at range, Amil's volume takes over.
- **Skid pressure** — desperation could push Rodriguez to brawl rather than wrestle, which is the losing game plan.
- **Apex small-cage** can aid both pressure striking (Amil) and cage wrestling (Rodriguez) — net wash.

## 13. Risk Factors & Confidence Assessment
### Primary Risks to Rodriguez Winning
1. He fights to his ego on the feet instead of wrestling and gets out-volumed/clipped (Amil has KO power).
2. His own low output costs him close rounds if takedowns don't stick.
3. Variance — a switch-stance volume striker with power can end any round.

### Confidence Level
Style and the Emmers comp clearly favor Rodriguez; he is rightly the favorite. **Model estimate: ~62% Rodriguez.** Against an implied ~60%, the edge is only ~+2%, below the 5% moneyline threshold. Lean Rodriguez on the merits but the price is roughly fair — no strong ML value; method/prop is the more interesting angle.
