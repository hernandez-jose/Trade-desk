## Fighting Style

**Elite kickboxer transitioning to complete MMA fighter — pace, precision, and deceptive power**

Kavanagh is a kickboxer by foundation — a black belt and five-time K-1 World Champion before transitioning to MMA at 19. He has built an MMA game around his elite striking base, adding solid BJJ (purple belt) and exceptional takedown defense. His style is characterized by explosive in-and-out movement, combination attacks that mix punches and kicks fluidly, and a pace that pressures opponents without overcommitting. He is a sports science graduate and trains at Great Britain Top Team under head coach Brad Pickett (former UFC bantamweight contender), which has given him a technical foundation rare for a fighter his age.

### Grappling
- Purple belt in BJJ; functional but not a submission threat
- Takedown defense is elite (89%) — the best facet of his grappling game
- Against Moreno, defended all 8 takedown attempts over 5 rounds — a statement performance
- When taken down or against the fence, he can struggle to create separation in extended clinch exchanges
- Offensive wrestling is limited (1.79 TD avg/15 min) — not a weapon he relies on
- Submission attempts are rare; prefers to scramble back to feet

### Striking
- His calling card: combination striking behind a bouncing, rhythmic movement pattern
- Rapid-fire hands combined with lead leg kicks and body work
- Five-time K-1 World Champion — elite kickboxing pedigree
- Counter-striking tendency — lures opponents into exchanges, times them
- Deceptive power for 125 lbs — has scored early stoppages with a single left hook (An Tuan Ho DWCS) and spinning back kick (Scarano)
- Does not telegraph shots; unpredictable attack sequences
- **Vulnerability**: Has shown susceptibility to hard right hands mid-combination — Johnson landed a right hook that instantly ended him in Round 2 (UFC Fight Night 257)

### Cardio
Kavanagh showed his cardio ceiling in the Moreno fight — five competitive rounds against a former two-time champion at a relentless pace. He maintained sharpness, output, and footwork from Round 1 to Round 5. The jury is still out on how he performs when genuinely hurt and running on fumes, but the Moreno performance was the most telling data point yet.

## Career Defining Fights

### vs. An Tuan Ho — DWCS S8 W1 (Win, KO R1, 2:35)
The fight that made his name. Kavanagh put a leaping left hook on the chin of the previously undefeated Ho, dropping him instantly for a prolonged stoppage. Dana White reportedly wrote "holy sh*t" on his scoring sheet. The raw power and timing showcased here set immediate expectations for his UFC arrival.

### vs. Charles Johnson — UFC Fight Night 257 (Loss, KO R2, 4:35)
The one blemish — and a significant one. Kavanagh was outperforming Johnson in the early rounds when Johnson landed a right hook that immediately erased his consciousness. The loss revealed a chin vulnerability: he went from dominant to finished in a single exchange. The lesson for bettors: he is stoppable by any flyweight with right-hand power.

### vs. Brandon Moreno — UFC Fight Night 268 (Win, UD 49-46, 48-47, 48-47)
The breakthrough. Short notice, hostile crowd in Mexico City, former two-time champion. Kavanagh controlled the fight with leg kicks that made Moreno limp from Round 2, bloodied the former champion, and defended 8 takedown attempts across 5 rounds. Judges scored it 49-46, 48-47, 48-47. Earned him #6 flyweight ranking and a $100,000 Performance of the Night bonus. This performance established him as a legitimate title contender, not just a prospect.
