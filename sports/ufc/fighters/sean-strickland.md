## Fighting Style

**High-volume orthodox pressure boxer with Philly Shell guard, elite chin, and world-class cardio**

### Stance and Defense

Strickland fights from a distinctive tall, upright orthodox stance with a Philly Shell guard — lead hand on lower torso, back hand near the jaw. Key defensive characteristics:

- **Shoulder roll**: absorbs incoming punches by rolling the shoulder, deflecting rather than blocking
- **Upright hips**: weight distributed evenly, allows instant leg kick checks
- **Forward pressure with defense**: advances behind the shell, throwing jabs while staying defensively covered
- **61% significant strike defense**: one of the highest rates among active UFC middleweights, comparable to Khabib Nurmagomedov (65%), above Israel Adesanya (56%)
- **Gets hit less than almost anyone**: Joe Rogan has noted Strickland's mouthpiece data shows historically low contact rate

### Offensive Striking

- **Jab-dominant**: primary weapon is the long jab thrown in high volume, setting up the straight right
- **Constant forward pressure**: forces opponents into boxing range by walking them down
- **Volume over power**: throws 5–7+ significant strikes per minute; does not prioritize KO
- **Near-zero kick game**: almost exclusively hands; opponents can use leg kicks relatively freely
- **No urgency to finish**: will land 200+ strikes over 5 rounds without hunting for the KO — a stylistic choice that costs him in close decisions

### Grappling

- **Not an offensive wrestler**: takedown attempts are infrequent and not a gameplan staple
- **Strong takedown defense (76–78%)**: prefers to keep fights standing; gets back to his feet efficiently when taken down
- **Zero submission offense**: has not submitted a UFC opponent; this prop is a hard fade
- **Chinny on the ground? No** — has survived difficult positions

### Cardio / Conditioning

- Widely considered top-3 cardio in UFC middleweight history
- Round 5 pace is indistinguishable from Round 1
- Opponents who front-load aggression often visibly fade vs. Strickland late
- Trained extensively at Xtreme Couture; known for extreme sparring volume

## Career Defining Fights (Narrative)

### vs. Alex Pereira — UFC 276 (Loss, KO R1)
Knocked cold by the Brazilian kickboxing legend in the first round. Pereira hit him with a right hand that ended it in 2:36. One of only two KO losses in his career. Identified power punchers as a structural vulnerability.

### vs. Jared Cannonier — UFC FN (Loss, UD3) Dec 2022
Lost a three-round decision in a performance that looked shaky. Coming off the Pereira KO, this raised questions about his long-term trajectory.

### vs. Israel Adesanya — UFC 293 (Win, UD5) — Middleweight Title
The defining upset. Strickland entered at +450 to +490 odds (closed approximately +450 on most books), one of the biggest upsets in middleweight history. He dominated "The Last Stylebender" with relentless forward pressure and volume boxing. Adesanya never found his range. Shocked the MMA world.

### vs. Dricus du Plessis — UFC 297 (Loss, SD5)
Lost the title on a razor-close split decision. Extremely controversial; Strickland landed more total strikes. Du Plessis landed the more damaging shots. First clear example of his refusal-to-finish costing him a championship.

### vs. Paulo Costa — UFC 302 (Win, SD3)
Entered as -245 to -265 favorite; won by split decision over 3 rounds. A grind — showed his ability to win even when not at his best, but exposed how close his decisions can be even as a heavy favorite.

### vs. Dricus du Plessis — UFC 312 (Loss, UD5) Feb 2025
The rematch went decisively to du Plessis. Du Plessis broke Strickland's nose and outworked him, earning a clean sweep of the scorecards. Less controversial than the first fight — du Plessis clearly outperformed him.

### vs. Anthony Hernandez — UFC Houston (Win, TKO R3) Feb 2026
Career comeback performance. Strickland beat Hernandez to the punch throughout, dominated the standup, and earned his first stoppage finish since 2023. Stopped Hernandez's 8-fight win streak. Immediately called out Khamzat Chimaev, earned the title shot.

## Summary: Key Betting Angles

1. **Never bet Strickland by early KO or submission** — his wins come late and on the scorecards
2. **Always consider the over on totals** — he does not get finished; fights go the distance
3. **Large underdog prices (+300+) are historically profitable** — Adesanya proved books misprize his durability
4. **Close decisions are a coin flip** — he lands more strikes but doesn't always get the nod
5. **Against elite grapplers: bet cautiously** — his takedown defense is good but not proven vs. Chimaev-level wrestlers
6. **Five-round fights are his domain** — cardio is the great equalizer; he always has more left in the tank
