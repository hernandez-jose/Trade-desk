## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR:** Raposo (10-3, 1-2 UFC) is a switch-stance fighter with a genuine amateur pedigree — state-champ wrestler, undefeated amateur kickboxer, trained BJJ — and four first-round finishes on the regional scene. At 27 he's seven years younger than Nascimento and has never been blown out (both UFC losses are split decisions). If he keeps the fight standing and uses footwork/counters, he can out-point a grappler whose striking defense is only ~50%. **AGAINST:** He gives up ~3" height and ~5" reach, fights at a low/counter-reliant volume that has cost him two split decisions, has a career submission loss, and is stepping in against the most dangerous submission artist he's faced. He was also forced off the April 18 card by illness — a question mark on this camp. **Most likely path:** Win = a disciplined range-and-movement decision; lose = dragged into a scramble and submitted, or out-grappled to a decision.

## 3. Opponent Context
Nascimento is a 22-6 submission specialist (15 sub wins, ~80% finish) with a real size/reach edge. The threat to Raposo is the scramble and the neck — any failed/reactive takedown or sloppy exchange can become a front-headlock submission. Raposo's counter-and-move style is double-edged here: staying at range protects him, but his own surprise takedown attempts hand Nascimento entries into the grappling. The opportunity for Raposo: Nascimento's ~50% striking defense and poor reported TDD mean a disciplined kickboxing/range game can win rounds without grappling at all.
