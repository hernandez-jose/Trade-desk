# Ciryl Gane — Deep Fight Research
## UFC Freedom 250 | vs. Alex Pereira | June 14, 2026 | Heavyweight (Interim Title) | 5 Rounds

> Analyst note: Gane is the best mover and one of the best five-round gas tanks at heavyweight, facing a low-volume one-shot puncher making his HW debut. His path is clear and proven: stick, move, out-volume, win a decision. His risk is equally clear: he carries little power, has historically frozen against punchers (Ngannou), and is fighting a man who only needs one clean shot across 25 minutes. The model leans Gane on *who controls the engagement*, but it is a coin flip with real downside.
> Last updated: June 2026

---

## 1. Executive Summary
**The case FOR Gane:** Elite footwork (10/10), the best defensive striking at heavyweight (2.25 SApM, 63% defense), and a genuine five-round cardio tank. He can fight on the outside, out-touch Pereira with volume (5.11 SLpM at 60% accuracy), and win a points decision against a notoriously low-output opponent. He is the natural heavyweight and the more experienced fighter at 265. Pereira is debuting at HW at age 38.

**The case AGAINST Gane:** Two recurring flaws — (1) **low knockout power**, meaning he likely can't finish or deter Pereira, and (2) a tendency to fight tentatively against heavy hitters (he never let his hands go vs Ngannou and lost a fight he was favored in). His **56% takedown defense** is a hole, but Pereira is not the wrestler to exploit it. The real danger is that he must avoid one Pereira shot for 25 minutes, and history says Pereira eventually lands.

**Most likely path:** Decision via movement and volume; secondary risk of getting clipped and finished. ## 3. Opponent Context
Pereira is a stylistic threat precisely where Gane is vulnerable: a fight-ending puncher against a man with low power who must win on movement and volume. If Gane plays it perfectly (constant lateral movement, range jab, occasional level change), Pereira's low output and 50% striking defense let Gane bank rounds. The danger is two-fold: Pereira's calf kick can erode the movement that is Gane's entire game, and Pereira's one-shot power means a single lapse ends the night. Gane's underused wrestling (0.62 TD/15) is a tool he *could* deploy to steal control rounds against Pereira's untested-at-HW takedown defense — a path most analysts overlook.

---

## 4. Striking Analysis
### Offense
High-volume, range-based, technically clean (5.11 SLpM, 60% accuracy). Jab, push kicks, kicks to the body/legs, stance switches. The glaring caveat: **little one-punch power for a heavyweight** — his finishes come from accumulation (Lewis, Spivac) not single shots.

### Defense
Best in the division. 2.25 SApM and 63% defense — he simply doesn't get hit much because he's never standing still. This is the foundation of any winning game plan vs Pereira.

### Historical Striking Data
SLpM 5.11, accuracy 60%, SApM 2.25, defense 63% ([UFCStats](http://ufcstats.com/fighter-details/787bb1f087ccff8a)). The volume + evasion profile is exactly what beats a low-output puncher on the cards.

## 5. Grappling Analysis
### Offensive Wrestling
Light and underused (0.62 TD/15, 21% accuracy). Capable of a reactive takedown to mix looks, but not a wrestling-led fighter. Against Pereira this is an *underrated* card-stealing tool.

### Defensive Wrestling
The hole: 56% TD defense. Jon Jones took him down at will and submitted him in R1 (UFC 285). Irrelevant here — Pereira does not shoot — but it remains his structural weakness.

### Scrambles & Submissions
Has 3 career submission wins; competent on top. He could conceivably take Pereira down and grind, though it's out of character.

### Cage Wrestling
Adequate but not a strength; he prefers open space, which also suits a fight against a non-wrestler.

## 6. Cardio & Durability
### Pace Sustainability
A genuine asset — he has gone the full 25 minutes multiple times (Ngannou, Volkov x2) without fading. In a 5-round fight against an opponent with cardio questions carrying HW mass, this is a real late-fight edge (per the model's "+3 to +6% to the high-gas-tank fighter in 5 rounds").

### Recovery After Damage
Has rarely been badly hurt on the feet; not heavily tested by elite one-shot power, which is the open question vs Pereira.

### Weight Cut Effects
Minimal — comfortable natural heavyweight, weighed 248. No depletion concern.

### Injury History
The Aspinall NC stemmed from poking Aspinall's eyes, not from Gane's own injury. No active injury reported for Gane; ~8-month layoff is the only flag.

## 10. Historical Context
### Style Matchup History
Gane beats grinders and aggressive forward pressure when he can use space (Lewis, Tuivasa, Volkov). He has lost when (a) a puncher made him passive (Ngannou) and (b) a wrestler controlled him (Jones). Pereira is a puncher but not a wrestler — so the relevant risk is the Ngannou-passivity pattern.

### Comparable Opponent Analysis
No prior opponent combines Pereira's exact profile (elite one-shot KO power + calf kicks + low volume). Ngannou is the closest power comp, and Gane lost that one by failing to engage — the key lesson he must avoid repeating.

### Career Arc
36 years old, in his physical prime for a heavyweight, with a third crack at heavyweight gold (interim) after falling short vs Ngannou and Jones.

## 11. Key Paths to Victory
### Path 1: Stick-and-move decision (HIGH likelihood, ~55–60% of his win equity)
Constant lateral movement, range jab/kicks, never plant in front of the power side. Out-volume Pereira and bank a clear 48-47 / 49-46 decision. His +275 decision prop captures this.

### Path 2: Movement + reactive takedowns (MODERATE)
Mix in level changes to steal control rounds against Pereira's untested HW TD defense — an underrated, high-percentage way to lock up a decision.

### Path 3: Late TKO by accumulation (LOW)
If Pereira fades in rounds 4–5, Gane piles on volume for a stoppage. Possible but not his power profile.

## 12. X-Factors
- **~8-month layoff + inconclusive last fight (NC)** — minor ring-rust flag; he took no damage but hasn't had a clean competitive look since Volkov II (Dec 2024).
- **Cage size / outdoor White House setup** — a larger cage massively helps his movement game; confirm dimensions.
- **Calf-kick vulnerability** — if Pereira chops the lead leg early, Gane's entire movement-based plan degrades.
- **Mental engagement** — the single biggest swing factor: aggressive-enough Gane wins comfortably; tentative Gane loses a fight he should win.

## 13. Risk Factors & Confidence Assessment
### Primary Risks to Gane Winning
1. Gets clipped by one Pereira shot — he only has to be caught once in 25 minutes.
2. Repeats the Ngannou pattern and fights too passively to win rounds.
3. Calf kicks erode his footwork and neutralize his only structural edge.

### Confidence Level
The model leans Gane on the controlling-the-engagement question (a tireless mover dictating range over 5 rounds vs a low-volume puncher), with a small 5-round cardio bonus, partially offset by ring rust and Pereira's standalone finishing threat. **Model estimate for Gane: ~52–54%.** Best expression is the **decision prop (~+275)** rather than a near-pick'em moneyline.
