## 1. Executive Summary
**FOR:** Nascimento is 22-6 with a ~80% finish rate and 15 submission wins — a genuine grappling threat at flyweight, with a notable size/reach edge (5'8", 69.5" vs. Raposo's 5'5", 64.5"). He is the more experienced UFC fighter, on a win streak (dec over Jafel Filho, anaconda-choke sub over Cody Durden), and faces an opponent who carries a career submission loss and counter-heavy, low-volume tendencies. **AGAINST:** His takedown numbers are pedestrian (1.55 TD/15min, 26% accuracy) and reported TDD is poor (16%, small sample); his striking defense is only ~50%. If Raposo uses footwork to keep it at range and pot-shots, three rounds can slip away on the cards — Raposo's UFC fights have all gone the distance. **Most likely path:** Nascimento closes distance/clinch, drags it to the mat in a scramble, and attacks the neck — submission or grappling-control decision.

*(Sources: UFC.com, ESPN, Tapology, Odds Shark)*

## 3. Opponent Context
Raposo (10-3, 1-2 UFC) is a switch-stance counter-striker with a wrestling base. He prefers range, lateral movement and reactive takedowns. The danger to Nascimento is Raposo simply refusing to engage in grappling and outpointing him over 15 minutes — Raposo's two losses were both competitive split decisions, so he is hard to put away and stays in close fights. The opportunity: Raposo has a career submission loss and his counter style sags on output; if Nascimento closes distance, the scramble/sub game heavily favors him.
