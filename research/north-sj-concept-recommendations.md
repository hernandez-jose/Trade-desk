# North San Jose — Restaurant Concept Recommendations
*Built from live market research, competitor analysis, and demographic data. See reference files: [north-sj-market-analysis.md](north-sj-market-analysis.md) and [north-sj-competitor-landscape.md](north-sj-competitor-landscape.md)*

## How This Was Built

This document scores 7 restaurant concepts against the specific conditions of the North San Jose market:
- Demographics: 56,790 residents + large daytime worker population; median HHI $151K+; 51.9% Asian; 87% professional/executive employment; median age 33
- Competition: Weak lunch category; Korean and Indian dominant but underperforming; no health-forward bowl concept; no quality breakfast
- Real estate: $2.50–$4.33/sqft/mo NNN — favorable
- Customer behavior: Heavy delivery, strong corporate catering demand, tech-native ordering habits
- Labor: California minimum wage; lean AI-augmented staffing essential

Each concept is scored 1–5 on six dimensions:
- **Market fit** — How well does the concept match North SJ's demographics and gaps?
- **Unit economics** — Can the concept hit 65% prime cost or below?
- **Defensibility** — How hard is it for a competitor to copy and displace you?
- **Catering upside** — Does the format lend itself to corporate catering?
- **Delivery viability** — Does the food travel well? Does the concept work as a ghost kitchen?
- **Operator complexity** — Lower score = simpler to run; higher score = harder

## Concept #1: Health-Forward Pan-Asian Bowl
*The Highest-Conviction Recommendation*

### What It Is
A fast casual built around customizable bowls that draw on Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, and Southeast Asian flavors — think grain bases (brown rice, quinoa, soba), choice of protein (teriyaki chicken, bulgogi beef, miso-glazed salmon, tofu), fresh toppings, and house sauces. Roughly: if Sweetgreen and a Japanese bento merged. Average check $18–$23. Add a matcha/tea/coffee bar for beverage margin.

**The closest national comp:** Sweetgreen + Cava — neither has a North San Jose location.

### Why North San Jose Specifically
- 51.9% Asian population has instant authenticity recognition and high standards — they'll tell you if you get it wrong and champion you loudly if you get it right
- The tech worker lunch persona (age 25–40, health-conscious, time-constrained) is the single best customer for a customizable bowl concept
- No equivalent exists in the corridor — the nearest comparable is downtown (15 min away)
- Bowl format travels well for delivery; excellent for corporate catering (individual boxes, easy to customize for dietary restrictions)
- Indian vegetarian, Korean halal, Japanese pescatarian — all served by one concept

### Concept Details
**Name idea:** Basho / Kin / Miso & Co. (test names — workshop with AI)
**Format:** Fast casual; order-at-counter; in-person + delivery + catering
**Size:** 900–1,400 sqft
**Seats:** 30–45
**Average check:** $19–$23 (in-house); $22–$27 (delivery with packaging fee)
**Operating hours:** 10:30am–8pm Mon–Sat; closed Sunday
**Primary revenue streams:**
1. Lunch dine-in / takeout (50% of revenue)
2. Delivery via DoorDash/Uber Eats (25%)
3. Corporate catering — EZCater + direct accounts (25%)

### Target Location
- **First choice:** Zanker Rd corridor (95134) near Cisco/Intel; capture the non-campus workers and Cisco employees leaving for variety
- **Second choice:** North First St (95131) near BART for commuter + residential capture

### Menu Structure (Simple)
```
BASE: Brown rice / White rice / Soba noodles / Mixed greens / Quinoa
PROTEIN: Teriyaki chicken / Bulgogi beef / Miso salmon / Tofu / Shrimp
TOPPINGS (choose 4): Edamame / Cucumber / Pickled daikon / Avocado /
                     Roasted sweet potato / Kimchi / Crispy garlic / Corn
SAUCE: Sesame-ginger / Gochujang-lime / Ponzu / Miso-tahini / Spicy yuzu
ADD-ONS: Soft egg / Nori / Tempura crunch (+$1.50)
DRINKS: Matcha latte / Houji-cha / Sparkling yuzu / Still water
```
14 items total at launch. Expand at 90 days based on sales data.

### Food Cost Target
- Average bowl cost to make: $5.50–$7.00
- Menu price: $19–$22
- Blended food cost: 29–32% ✓

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 5/5 | Direct answer to the identified gap; serves the #1 demographic |
| Unit economics | 4/5 | Bowl format has strong margin; beverage add-ons boost it further |
| Defensibility | 4/5 | Differentiated by authenticity + execution; concept can be copied but culture cannot |
| Catering upside | 5/5 | Individual boxes; allergen-customizable; exactly what tech companies want |
| Delivery viability | 4/5 | Bowls travel well; sauce separate; 20-min hold is fine |
| Operator complexity | 3/5 | Multiple proteins and toppings require good mise en place discipline |
| **TOTAL** | **25/30** | |

### Ghost Kitchen Entry Path
Launch as a ghost kitchen at SalTalk (3334 Victor Ct, Santa Clara) for 6 months. Run exclusively on DoorDash + Uber Eats + EZCater. Validate:
- Which bowl combinations are most ordered
- Average check size
- Delivery radius that generates orders
- Corporate catering repeat rate

Use that 6-month data to negotiate a lease on a Zanker Rd inline space. Enter the brick-and-mortar phase with real order history, not projections.

## Concept #2: Premium Vietnamese Fast Casual
*The Underserved Community Play*

### What It Is
A fast casual or counter-service Vietnamese restaurant that brings the quality of the best Little Saigon phở and bánh mì shops to North San Jose — where this community has almost no quality options. Not generic "pho restaurant" — positioned with a clean, modern aesthetic, strong social media presence, and a menu that goes beyond pho to include vermicelli bowls (bún), grilled meats, Vietnamese iced coffee, and fresh spring rolls. Average check $14–$18.

### Why North San Jose Specifically
- Large Vietnamese diaspora in the broader SJ area but Little Saigon (Tully/Senter area) is 20–30 minutes from North SJ
- North SJ workers of Vietnamese descent are currently driving to other neighborhoods or settling for inferior options
- Vietnamese food also has extremely broad mainstream appeal — it is one of the most popular delivery cuisines in Silicon Valley
- Food cost is favorable: pho, vermicelli, and spring rolls all have low ingredient costs at scale

### Concept Details
**Format:** Fast casual / counter service
**Size:** 800–1,200 sqft
**Average check:** $15–$19
**Hours:** 10am–8pm Mon–Sat; 10am–6pm Sun
**Primary revenue:** Lunch dine-in (60%) + delivery (30%) + catering (10% to start, scale)

### Menu Structure
```
SOUPS: Phở Bò (beef, 4 varieties) / Phở Gà (chicken) / Bún bò Huế / Mì Quảng
BOWLS: Bún thịt nướng (grilled pork vermicelli) / Bún gà nướng / Gỏi (salads)
BÁNH MÌ: 6 varieties (classic, lemongrass chicken, tofu, pâté)
STARTERS: Chả giò (spring rolls) / Gỏi cuốn (fresh rolls) / Canh chua (soup)
DRINKS: Cà phê sữa đá (Vietnamese iced coffee) / Sinh tố (smoothies) / Boba
SIDES: Cơm tấm (broken rice) upgrades
```

### Food Cost Target
- Pho bowl: ~$3.50–$4.50 to make; sell for $14–$17 → 26–32% food cost ✓
- Bánh mì: ~$2.50–$3.50 to make; sell for $11–$13 → 27–32% ✓
- Vietnamese iced coffee: ~$0.60 to make; sell for $5.50 → 11% ✓ (high margin beverage)

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 5/5 | Directly serves an underserved community with proven high demand |
| Unit economics | 5/5 | Vietnamese food has some of the best margins in the category |
| Defensibility | 3/5 | Category is established; differentiation is quality and experience |
| Catering upside | 4/5 | Pho catering is a real thing (large pots); bánh mì catering is excellent for offices |
| Delivery viability | 3/5 | Pho travels poorly if broth sits; bánh mì and bowls travel well; separate broth for delivery |
| Operator complexity | 3/5 | Broth requires hours of cooking; execution is demanding but learnable |
| **TOTAL** | **23/30** | |

### Competitive Note
There is no high-quality Vietnamese fast casual in North SJ. The barrier is finding an operator with genuine Vietnamese cooking expertise — this concept requires authentic technique, not just a theme. The right operator has a significant moat.

## Concept #3: Indian Tiffin & Corporate Catering Hub
*The B2B Play — Highest Margin Potential*

### What It Is
A lunch-focused Indian tiffin (meal box) concept that targets corporate catering as its primary revenue stream from day one, with a small walk-in counter as secondary. The menu is built around familiar but elevated Indian dishes — dal, sabzi, roti, biryani, raita — packed in beautiful stainless or recyclable tiffin boxes. Available for corporate delivery in boxes of 10, 20, 50. A daily rotating 5-dish menu keeps it fresh and simple. Average catering order: $18–$22/person.

This is a direct counter-play to Curry Up Now's catering business but positioned on **authenticity vs. fusion** and **quality portions vs. small plates**.

### Why North San Jose Specifically
- Indian engineers are one of the largest demographic groups at Cisco, Google, Adobe, and across Silicon Valley tech — there is genuine cultural appetite and quality expectations
- Vegetarian-first menu has broad appeal across Hindu, Buddhist, and plant-based tech workers
- Curry Up Now's catering dominates but has complaints — room for a better-quality entrant
- The tiffin format is culturally resonant and has strong Instagram/social appeal (visual, distinctive)

### Concept Details
**Format:** Ghost kitchen to start → small counter location at 12 months
**Primary revenue:** Corporate catering via EZCater + direct accounts
**Secondary:** Walk-in / delivery for individuals
**Menu model:** Daily rotating 5 dishes (e.g., Monday: Palak Paneer + Dal Makhani + Jeera Rice + Roti + Raita) — announced each morning on Instagram/email
**Average corporate order:** $18–$22/person × 30 people = $540–$660 per order
**Target:** 5 corporate accounts ordering 2x/week = $27,000–$33,000/month in catering alone

### Food Cost Target
- Indian vegetarian thali cost: ~$3.50–$5.50 to make; sell at $17–$22 → 25–32% ✓
- One of the most favorable food cost profiles in the category

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 5/5 | Large Indian professional population; cultural authenticity resonates |
| Unit economics | 5/5 | Vegetarian ingredients = low food cost; catering orders are high margin |
| Defensibility | 4/5 | Authentic technique + cultural trust is hard to fake |
| Catering upside | 5/5 | This IS the catering concept — built for it |
| Delivery viability | 5/5 | Tiffin boxes are designed to travel; Indian food holds well |
| Operator complexity | 3/5 | Rotating daily menu requires planning but reduces on-the-fly decisions |
| **TOTAL** | **27/30** | |

**This is the highest-scoring concept for a B2B-first strategy.** The ghost kitchen launch with zero build-out cost + corporate catering focus means you can reach profitability faster than any dine-in concept, and the Indian professional demographic in North SJ is the most reliable early adopter.

## Concept #4: Persian / Middle Eastern Fast Casual
*The Underserved Niche with Outsized Loyalty*

### What It Is
An authentic Persian fast casual offering kababs, stews (khoresht), rice dishes, dips, and fresh flatbreads — served in a fast-casual format. Think: the quality of a sit-down Persian restaurant, the speed and price of Chipotle. Average check $17–$22.

**The gap:** SAJJ (Mediterranean) is the only comparable option near the Cisco corridor, and it is a simplified franchise version of Middle Eastern food. There is no authentic Persian restaurant in North San Jose.

### Why North San Jose Specifically
- Persian/Iranian-Americans are a significant demographic in Silicon Valley's engineering workforce — Cisco, Broadcom, and Nvidia have large Iranian-American engineering populations
- This community has high disposable income and very strong cultural loyalty to authentic Persian food
- Persian cuisine is almost entirely unfamiliar to the broader tech worker population — which creates both a discovery opportunity (it's novel!) and a risk (education required)
- The cuisine is naturally health-forward: lean proteins (lamb, chicken, beef kabab), herb-forward salads, legume-heavy stews — perfect for the health-conscious tech worker

### Concept Details
**Format:** Fast casual
**Size:** 800–1,100 sqft
**Average check:** $18–$23
**Hours:** 11am–8pm Mon–Sat

### Menu (Simple Launch)
```
KABABS: Koobideh (ground beef/lamb) / Joojeh (saffron chicken) / Barg (beef tenderloin)
STEWS: Ghormeh Sabzi / Fesenjan / Khoresh Karafs (on rotation, 2 daily)
RICE: Chelo (steamed) / Tahdig (crispy rice, limited) / Basmati with saffron
SIDES: Shirazi salad / Mast-o-khiar (yogurt + cucumber) / Hummus / Lavash
DRINKS: Doogh (Persian yogurt drink) / Sharbat / Persian chai
```

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 4/5 | Serves an underserved community; broader appeal requires education |
| Unit economics | 4/5 | Kababs have good margin; rice dishes are high margin |
| Defensibility | 5/5 | Authentic Persian is extremely hard to fake; community trust is the moat |
| Catering upside | 4/5 | Kabab trays for events are culturally embedded; large-format orders are natural |
| Delivery viability | 3/5 | Rice and kabab travel moderately well; tahdig does not |
| Operator complexity | 3/5 | Kabab grilling is skilled labor; stews require long prep |
| **TOTAL** | **23/30** | |

## Concept #5: Tech Campus Breakfast & Coffee
*Filling the Morning Void*

### What It Is
A premium all-day breakfast and coffee concept that opens at 6:30am and serves until 3pm. Menu: specialty coffee (espresso, matcha, filter), breakfast sandwiches, egg bowls, avocado toast, acai bowls, and fresh pastries. Format: counter service, fast, bright, clean. Average check $14–$20 (coffee + food combo).

### Why North San Jose Specifically
- There is virtually no quality breakfast option in North SJ near the tech campuses
- Engineers on-campus at Cisco start at 7–8am; meetings start at 9am; breakfast from the cafeteria is the only current option
- Specialty coffee is not adequately covered in the corridor (no Blue Bottle, Philz, or equivalent)
- Breakfast has the best margins per labor hour of any daypart (fastest turns, lowest staffing, highest beverage attachment)

### Concept Details
**Format:** Counter service
**Size:** 600–900 sqft (minimal seating; designed for grab-and-go)
**Hours:** 6:30am–3pm Mon–Fri (captures the full tech work day)
**Average check:** $15–$19 (most orders include coffee + food)
**Coffee margin:** A $6 latte costs ~$0.90 to make (15% food cost) — among the highest margins in the food business

### Menu Structure
```
COFFEE: Espresso drinks / Filter coffee / Matcha / Hojicha / Chai
TEA: Loose-leaf and cold brew teas
BREAKFAST SANDWICHES: 5 varieties (egg + cheese / bacon egg / veggie / smoked salmon)
BOWLS: Acai / Granola / Egg bowl with greens
TOAST: Avocado toast / Nut butter
PASTRIES: 4–6 rotating items (croissant, muffin, scone) from a local wholesale baker
LUNCH (11am–3pm): 2–3 sandwiches and a soup rotation
```

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 5/5 | Fills a genuine void; no breakfast competition in the corridor |
| Unit economics | 5/5 | Coffee margins are extraordinary; breakfast has low labor cost |
| Defensibility | 3/5 | Breakfast concepts can be copied quickly; coffee brand loyalty is strong but fragile |
| Catering upside | 3/5 | Breakfast catering is real (bagels + coffee for morning meetings) but limited scale |
| Delivery viability | 2/5 | Coffee doesn't travel; breakfast is grab-and-go — weak delivery story |
| Operator complexity | 2/5 | Simplest concept on the list; close by 3pm |
| **TOTAL** | **20/30** | |

**Note:** Lower overall score but the highest simplicity — ideal for a first-time operator or as an add-on to an existing concept. The 6:30am–3pm schedule also means normal human hours for the owner.

## Concept #6: Korean Fried Chicken & Soju Bar
*The Evening Anchor Concept*

### What It Is
A Korean fried chicken spot that operates as a fast casual at lunch and transforms into a soju + beer bar with shared plates in the evening. Two revenue streams, one kitchen. Think: Noori Chicken meets a young Korean pojangmacha vibe. Average lunch check $16–$20. Average dinner check $35–$45 with drinks.

### Why North San Jose
- Korean fried chicken is the fastest-growing Asian food segment in the US
- There is no soju bar in North SJ — the evening dining void is real
- The young (median age 33), Asian-majority professional demographic is the exact target for this concept
- KFC + soju is an extremely photographable, shareable concept — strong organic social

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 4/5 | Strong for the demographic; evening options are very scarce |
| Unit economics | 4/5 | Alcohol dramatically improves margin in the evening |
| Defensibility | 4/5 | Culture + execution creates loyalty; hard to replicate the vibe |
| Catering upside | 2/5 | KFC trays can cater but limited corporate appeal vs. healthier options |
| Delivery viability | 3/5 | KFC travels adequately for 15–20 min; wings/tenders hold well |
| Operator complexity | 4/5 | Liquor license required; fry stations require skill and safety |
| **TOTAL** | **21/30** | |

**Requires:** Type 41 or 47 ABC license (3–6 months); higher build-out for bar infrastructure.

## Concept #7: Ghost Kitchen Multi-Brand (Highest Speed to Market)
*The Validation-First Strategy*

### What It Is
Launch 2–3 virtual brands from a single ghost kitchen, each targeting a different meal occasion on DoorDash and Uber Eats. Example portfolio:
- **Brand A:** Pan-Asian bowls (concept #1 in delivery format)
- **Brand B:** Vietnamese bánh mì + noodles (concept #2)
- **Brand C:** Breakfast bowls and coffee (limited hours via EZCater morning catering)

One kitchen, one team, three storefronts. Test demand data for all three simultaneously. Double down on whichever brand wins.

### Scorecard
| Dimension | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market fit | 4/5 | Covers multiple gaps simultaneously |
| Unit economics | 5/5 | No build-out; shared labor; near-zero fixed cost |
| Defensibility | 2/5 | Ghost kitchen brands are easy to copy; must move to brick-and-mortar once validated |
| Catering upside | 4/5 | Can run EZCater for all brands |
| Delivery viability | 5/5 | This IS the model |
| Operator complexity | 4/5 | Managing 3 menus simultaneously adds complexity; requires systems |
| **TOTAL** | **24/30** | |

## Recommended Path: Two-Phase Entry

### Phase 1 (Months 1–9): Ghost Kitchen — Indian Tiffin + Pan-Asian Bowl
Launch both concepts from SalTalk Kitchen in Santa Clara (3334 Victor Ct) simultaneously:
- Indian Tiffin on EZCater + direct LinkedIn outreach to 20 target companies
- Pan-Asian Bowl on DoorDash + Uber Eats

Measure: Which concept generates more repeat orders? Which gets better reviews? Which corporate accounts come back?

**Target: $30K–$50K/month combined by Month 6**

### Phase 2 (Months 9–18): Brick-and-Mortar — Winning Concept
Take the winning concept to a 900–1,200 sqft space on Zanker Rd (95134) or North First (95131).
Negotiate aggressively for TI allowance (landlords are motivated in North SJ vs. Santana Row).
Carry the ghost kitchen brand as a delivery-only operation in the other commissary space.

**Target: $70K–$110K/month at brick-and-mortar by Month 18**
