## Background & Credentials

- **BJJ Black Belt** under Rodrigo Cabral, Brazilian Fight Factory, Las Vegas
- **Combat Jiu-Jitsu World Champion** — elite submission grappling pedigree
- Started training jiu-jitsu ~11 years ago; transitioned to full MMA training after relocating to Las Vegas
- Won UFC contract on **Dana White's Contender Series Season 8 (Week 9)** by stopping Chasen Blair with a body-shot TKO in Round 2
- UFC debut at **UFC 312 (Du Plessis vs. Strickland 2)** — lost to Rongzhu by UD but earned **Fight of the Night bonus** — a rare honor for a debut loss, indicating the entertainment value and competitiveness of his performance

## Fighting Style

**Pressure-based grappler with knockout power**

Steele is built around two weapons that rarely coexist:
1. **Elite BJJ** — Combat Jiu-Jitsu world champion; threat from almost every position
2. **KO power** — 57% finish rate by TKO/KO is high for a grappling-first fighter

His path to victory: pressure forward, mix grappling threats with straight striking, look to either drag the fight to the ground or land a finishing shot when the opponent over-focuses on takedown defense.

### Grappling
- Black belt credentials are the real deal — Rodrigo Cabral is a respected coach at Brazilian Fight Factory
- Combat Jiu-Jitsu background means he's comfortable with strikes on the ground, making his ground game more dangerous than a pure sport BJJ practitioner
- Submission threat is live from back control, guard, and top position

### Striking
- Power in his hands — 4 TKO/KO wins suggests genuine finishing ability
- Body shot TKO of Chasen Blair on DWCS showed he sets up the body well
- Still developing his overall striking craft — the UFC level requires sharper head movement and footwork

### Cardio
- Three-round fights on the regional circuit do not tax the cardio the same way as UFC-level opponents
- The Rongzhu fight (FOTN) suggests he can pace through 3 rounds at UFC level — a positive early indicator
