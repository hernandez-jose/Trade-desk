## Why Tennis is a Sharp Bettor's Sport

Tennis offers unique edges:
- **In-play / live betting** is the deepest and most inefficient market in sports
- **Head-to-head (H2H) records** are meaningful and often underpriced by books
- **Surface splits** create massive pricing errors — a grass specialist on clay is a different player
- **Fatigue models** matter more than in team sports — one player carries every point
- **Retirement risk** is a unique tennis variable — backing a player who retires mid-match loses the bet

## Core Tennis Bet Types

### 1. Match Winner (Moneyline)
The standard bet — who wins the match. Odds reflect ranking, form, and surface.

### 2. Set Betting
Predict the exact scoreline (e.g., 2-0, 2-1 in best-of-3; 3-0, 3-1, 3-2 in Grand Slams).
- More juice, but significant value in lopsided matchups (the favorite winning 2-0 is often underpriced)
- Grand Slam 3-0 set bets on elite clay courters are historically +EV

### 3. Game Handicap (Spread)
Similar to a point spread — one player gets/gives games.  
Example: Djokovic -4.5 games (must win by 5+ total games across all sets combined).

### 4. Total Games Over/Under
Bet on whether the total games played will exceed or fall short of the posted total.
- Best-of-3: typical range is 19.5 to 22.5
- Best-of-5: typical range is 33.5 to 37.5
- Key factors: baseline sluggers (more games) vs. big servers (fewer games, holds easily)

### 5. First Set Winner
Who wins the first set. Often better odds than the match winner, and first sets create live betting opportunities.

### 6. In-Play / Live Betting
The most profitable tennis market. See the Live Betting section below.

## Surface Analysis — The Most Important Factor

A player's surface profile is the single biggest predictor of tennis betting edge.

### Clay
- Slower surface — rallies are longer, baseline players thrive
- Spin (especially heavy topspin) is amplified
- Big servers are disadvantaged — serve is less of a weapon
- Fitness and stamina matter most
- **Who thrives**: Rafa Nadal (GOAT on clay), Carlos Alcaraz, Novak Djokovic, Jannik Sinner, Iga Swiatek
- **Who fades**: Fast-court specialists with limited footwork (big servers, flat hitters)

### Hard (US Open, Australian Open, most Masters)
- Medium pace — balanced between servers and baseliners
- Most common surface, so rankings are most reflective of hard-court ability
- **Who thrives**: Well-rounded players; big servers have an edge on indoor hard
- **Who fades**: Extreme clay specialists who never trained on hard courts

### Grass (Wimbledon, Queen's)
- Fastest surface — serves dominate, rallies are shorter
- Low bounce = flat hitters and slicers thrive
- **Who thrives**: Big servers (Isner, Raonic), aggressive volleyers, slice-heavy players
- **Who fades**: Heavy topspin clay court players (the ball doesn't bounce up to their strike zone)

### Indoor Hard
- Fastest conditions in tennis — serve is a huge weapon
- Low attendance = less crowd pressure on underdog
- **Who thrives**: Big servers and aggressive ball-strikers
- **Who fades**: Defensive retriever types

## Key Betting Angles

### Surface Mismatch
This is the clearest edge in tennis betting. A grass specialist playing on clay is a different player.

**How to use it:**
1. Check where the tournament falls on the calendar
2. Identify players who over/underperform on that surface relative to their ranking
3. Back surface specialists when they're priced close to or behind a generalist

### H2H Record on Specific Surface
Head-to-head records are more meaningful in tennis than any other sport. But filter by surface:
- A player who is 1-5 H2H vs. Djokovic overall might be 1-1 on grass
- Books price the overall H2H; you play the surface-specific record

### Fatigue Model
Tennis players play multiple matches per week in tournaments. Fatigue compounds over:
- Number of sets played vs. opponent's number of sets
- Days since last match
- Travel schedule between tournaments

**Fatigue edge:**
- If Player A played a 3-set grueler yesterday and Player B had a walkover (retirement): Back B
- Late-round Grand Slam underdogs who had to fight through early rounds: fade on fatigue
- Players who just came from a Masters event 3 days ago: Monitor serve % decline

### Ranking vs. Current Form
ATP/WTA rankings use a 52-week rolling system. A player can be #8 in the world but in terrible form (defending lots of points from a big run last year).

**The Form vs. Rank edge:**
- Player ranked #8 but on a 2-win, 8-loss run in their last 10: fade regardless of ranking
- Player ranked #22 but won their last 3 tournaments: back at the inflated underdog price

### First-Round Specialists vs. Deep-Run Specialists
Some players thrive in the first round (fresh legs, focused on entry) but fade later.  
Others warm up slowly and peak in Round 3+.

For first-round bets: check recent first-round records specifically.  
For later rounds: favor players who've shown they grind through adversity.

## Live Betting Strategy

Tennis live betting is the most underexploited market in sports. Why:
- Momentum swings are measurable in real-time
- A player breaks serve and wins the first set but the live line barely adjusts
- Service games have predictable statistical patterns (hold % by player)

**Core live betting angles:**

### The Break-Back Spot
When a player loses their serve (gets broken), books often overreact and price them as a bigger underdog. But the player getting broken:
- Now serves at the start of the next game
- Has added motivation to break back
- Historical break-back rate: ~42% in the very next game after getting broken

**Play**: Back the broken player (at +EV live odds) to break back immediately.

### First Set Loser on Fast Surfaces
On fast surfaces (grass, indoor hard), losing the first set is a momentum killer but the second set odds for the first-set loser are often still +EV if they're the better player.

**Play**: If the favorite loses the first set cleanly on a fast surface, back them for the second set — they still have the serve advantage.

### Tiebreak Situations
When a set reaches 6-6, it goes to a tiebreak. Books price tiebreaks close to 50/50, but:
- Big servers win tiebreaks at a higher rate (short format, serve matters most)
- Mental game specialists (Djokovic, Sinner) win tiebreaks above expectation
- Identify who has the tiebreak edge and bet it when the set reaches 5-5

## Odds Format Reference

Tennis uses decimal odds on most international books (including Klashi likely):

```
Convert Decimal to American:
  Favorite (decimal < 2.0): American = −(1/(decimal−1) × 100)
  Example: 1.40 → −(1/0.40 × 100) = −250

  Underdog (decimal > 2.0): American = (decimal − 1) × 100
  Example: 3.20 → 2.20 × 100 = +220
```

## Retirement Risk Management

Tennis bettors must understand retirement risk:
- If a player retires mid-match, most books void the bet (no action)
- **Klashi rule**: Check their specific retirement policy — some books settle on the score at retirement
- Monitor injury news pre-match; if a player is nursing an issue, reduce bet size
- Avoid betting heavily on players with known physical concerns in long events

## Grand Slam Specific Strategy

Grand Slams are best-of-5 for men, best-of-3 for women. This changes the math:
- Best-of-5 neutralizes upsets — the better player wins more consistently (sharper market)
- Best-of-3 creates more variance — first-round upsets are common (more edge for sharp bettors)
- Women's Grand Slam first rounds: Higher upset rate than men's → underdog value is real
- Men's Grand Slam deep rounds: Elite players dominate → fade men's underdogs in QF/SF
